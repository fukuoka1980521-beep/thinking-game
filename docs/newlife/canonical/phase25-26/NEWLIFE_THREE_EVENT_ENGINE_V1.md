# NEW LIFE — three shared events V1

Phase 25 design experiment; no 30-day placement. These events strike **the same six people** and produce a visible town-level outcome. All personal and commercial particulars are fictional. The modest unit counts make direct questions answerable; they are not a business forecast.

## Shared proposed facts (system-owned for simulation)

Hina proposes a small trial of **20 plain scones at 280 yen and 10 cookie bags at 240 yen**. The 30 sale units include **12 reservations and 18 walk-in units**. These numbers do not include material or labor cost and cannot establish profit. The first draft sign says **「本日30点」**, omitting the reservation split. Hina both bakes and sells; pickup duty has no appointed owner. Fumiko posts the draft after her usual formal check; Yohei asks what “30” refers to, but at first his warning is taken as dismissal. Miyoko will serve her own café customers; her seating is not a free waiting area. Jin is contracted for a bounded setup job; nobody can volunteer his later hours. Daisuke's workshop can host a small pickup station only if he chooses; no automatic “yes.” Customer complaints are normal human frustration, not a villain or a disaster. The most the sign can promise is **30 total**, never “30 walk-ins.”

### Event 1 — the small joint trial

**Pressure:** one afternoon trial sale using the hall frontage, adjacent shops and the café. Competence and autonomy are threatened by real numbers and public scrutiny; relatedness matters when help is offered without being requested. Visible hooks: Which items are left? How are reservations marked? Who is receiving a customer while Hina is baking? Did the seating agreement really include the café?

| NPC | Wants | Fears | First action, unprompted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hina | See whether customers buy and understand the two products | Everyone treats a small batch as failure | Posts prices and the ambiguous “30”; asks M about taste after serving. |
| Yohei | Ensure a buyer can tell what is actually available | Being asked to honor another person's promise | Checks the reservation list, asks H “店頭は何点?” |
| Daisuke | Make the physical setup work | A temporary pickup promise turns permanent | Repairs a wobbling chair and jokes about queue; postpones venue answer. |
| Jin | Get tables placed safely in agreed hours | Being assigned an unlimited next shift | Marks walkway width and completes contracted setup. |
| Miyoko | Welcome new foot traffic without stopping café service | Her four free chairs become assumed town seating | Offers paid coffee pairing; quietly counts occupied tables. |
| Fumiko | Have a permitted, orderly trial | An unsigned responsibility lands with hall | Dates and posts the flyer; sets end time and notes who is present. |

**Clashes:** H–Y over meaning of “30”; M–F over whether café seats were promised; D–J over a makeshift table. **Help:** Jin moves a table for H within contracted time; M offers flavor feedback; Yohei marks a reservation split if H agrees. **Stays out:** D explicitly avoids promising his own venue, which itself has a cost. Player may buy, ask quantities, help label stock, say nothing, or be rude; no action is required. Event completes without player.

**End observable:** the small batch is offered and buyer interest exists, but demand, margin and service quality are different questions. An early correction can prevent many mistaken walk-ins. Even after correction, an earlier photo of the first sign can circulate; no new fixed sales total is invented. Observe what a customer believed and what they received. A reviewable thread remains: who owns a public promise?

### Event 2 — a preventable expectation gap and pickup bottleneck

**Trigger:** while 12 reserved units are held, a visitor arrives after seeing the first sign and believes 30 items were available without reservation. The 18 walk-in units may already be taken. Two arrivals ask for pickup while Hina is busy with the till; an unassigned pickup job causes delay. No one is injured, and a reservation is not secretly canceled. **Severity varies**: a corrected sign and assigned pickup reduce wasted trips and complaints; passive or antagonistic choices leave the old wording circulating. The factual gap and one person's frustration remain in every simulation, preserving the pressure event without forcing catastrophic failure.

Autonomy threatened: Hina may feel other people have taken her shop's wording; Miyoko's seat choice is overridden. Competence threatened: Hina and Fumiko's public clarity, Yohei's claim that “I warned you”, Jin's ability to finish in the contracted window. Relatedness threatened: public blame, social misreadings and unasked labor.

| NPC | Immediate independent reaction | Risk and possible second action |
|---|---|---|
| Hina | Initially explains “30 total” and becomes defensive | Can inspect actual visitor words and post “予約12／店頭18”, or retreat to arguing recipe quality. |
| Yohei | Lays out the reservation count without calling it fraud | May stand back with “言っただろ” or help correct the stock board. |
| Daisuke | Jokes once, then notices nobody owns pickups | Must answer yes/no on an actual time-limited use of his workshop; laughter alone cannot solve it. |
| Jin | Clears the blocked passage within the agreed job | Can ask for help for one move or decline unpaid extra work; cannot be silently conscripted. |
| Miyoko | Serves seated customers first | Says her café is not an open waiting room, or absorbs the spillover and loses future capacity. |
| Fumiko | Initially points to approval procedure | Has to amend a dated notice, identify who will call affected visitors, or suspend posting. |

Player actions may provide one observation, perform a bounded practical task, ask a blunt question, amplify blame, or ignore the whole scene. A kind sentence alone cannot erase the sign or make the café's workload disappear. NPC-to-NPC repair is available even after a harmful player move.

### Event 3 — what, if anything, should continue?

**Question:** after the trial and correction, should the street repeat a joint small trial, run independent offers, or pause? There is no predetermined festival payoff. A public meeting consumes recorded relationships, stock/complaint evidence, capacity and explicit consent. The player may speak or leave; NPCs vote, negotiate, refuse and carry out their own decisions.

**Decision facts:** who owns reservation wording; how 12 held units are shown; who handles pickup, who pays/chooses Jin's hours, whether M permits any seats and when, whether H wants a repeat, whether D offers his venue; whether the margin is known (it is **not** known until actual costs are assembled). No binding repeat sale is approved solely from turnover of 8,000 yen maximum. D's “no” and M's “no” are allowed; approval needs named responsibilities, not unanimity or therapy.

**Four legitimate outcome families:**

1. **Limited joint retry**: H consents, stock/wording and pickup owner are named, costs are to be checked before committing beyond one more small trial; M sets her own seating boundary, Jin chooses a paid bounded task, D chooses venue yes/no. This is the most collective *conditional* outcome, not instant success.
2. **Narrower trial without some shops**: H makes a smaller solo offer, the café and/or general store opt out, hall posts only an accurate neutral notice. Friends can still help one another privately.
3. **Pause for measurement**: the sign is corrected, harmed visitors can get an explanation, but capacity, margin or responsibility is unresolved. No new sale is promised. This is a valid responsible ending.
4. **Split after friction**: the joint project ends; individuals keep businesses open and some relationships stay strained. An apology does not compel a new shared project.

**Decision constraint:** `limited_joint_retry` requires H's choice, an accountable notice owner, a named pickup plan, consent for each person's labor/space, and a cost-check checkpoint. If any are absent, choose narrower trial or pause; if cooperation has collapsed, split. The **NPCs themselves** can establish these requirements in a passive-player world. The gate must not force agreement because the player was friendly.

**Forward curiosity created by the people:** Can H answer a hard stock question without feeling judged? Will Y go past “I warned you” to help? When will D finally say yes or no? Can J request a second pair of hands? Will M name her own limit before resentment? Can F amend her own sign and assign someone else to maintain it? The third event resolves only those changes actually earned; it does not reveal six private secrets.
