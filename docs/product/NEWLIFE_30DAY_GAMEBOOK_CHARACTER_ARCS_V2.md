# NEW LIFE 30-Day Gamebook — Character Arcs V2 (PHASE_24, Section 8)

Rebuilds `NEWLIFE_30DAY_GAMEBOOK_CHARACTER_ARCS_V1.md` around the required 7-field template (SEED /
CONTRADICTION / COMPLICATION / PLAYER INTERVENTION / DELAY / CALLBACK / PAYOFF), still the same MAIN
SIX cast from `NEWLIFE_CHARACTER_ROSTER_V2.md`, no new characters. V1's 8-field arcs (want/fear/
blind-spot/relationship/private-fact/change-possibility/day-1-silence) are folded into this shape
rather than deleted -- WANT+FEAR become the SEED, BLIND SPOT stays inside CONTRADICTION, PRIVATE
FACT becomes what DELAY is protecting. Provenance tags per `NEWLIFE_EPISODE_PROVENANCE_V2.md`.

## Hina, 26 — bakery owner-to-be [episodes ORIGINAL_FICTION]

- SEED: wants the shop to actually open and stay open, this time -- specifically because it didn't,
  last time.
- CONTRADICTION: fast and confident about baking specifics; goes vague and quiet the instant
  anything personal comes up. Also reads Yohei's default gruffness as personal rejection when it's
  really just his pattern with every newcomer -- a blind spot she can't see from inside it.
- COMPLICATION: the real reason the last shop closed was money, not "moving on" -- and admitting
  that means admitting the fear could repeat itself here.
- PLAYER INTERVENTION: noticing the money-pressure crack when it surfaces (not forceable early);
  later, choosing to just listen rather than offer a fix once she actually says it.
- DELAY: the true-reason reveal is gated on *earned* relationship evidence (see
  `NEWLIFE_30DAY_GAMEBOOK_STATE_MODEL_LEDGER_V2.md`'s `RELATIONSHIP_EVIDENCE` list for Hina), never
  on a raw visit count or turn count -- V1's exact failure mode (structural visits felt like
  progress but didn't count) is the reason this field exists at all.
- CALLBACK: whichever concrete piece of relationship evidence unlocked the reveal is referenced
  specifically in how she opens up (e.g. "you actually helped carry that box" is a different callback
  line than "you asked about my goal once, and remembered").
- PAYOFF: the shop opens by the festival, or it doesn't and stays honestly, non-punitively open past
  Day 30 -- both real, both dignified, decided by accumulated evidence, never a coin flip.

## Daisuke, 45 — barber [E17/E23 REAL_OWNER_DERIVED, other episodes ORIGINAL_FICTION]

- SEED: has been sitting on a renovation decision for three months, not out of laziness but
  avoidance of what deciding would mean admitting.
- CONTRADICTION: draws everyone else out effortlessly mid-haircut; goes stiff and deflects the
  instant the same question points back at him.
- COMPLICATION: a second, smaller version of the same avoidance habit -- an unanswered New Year's
  card to a friend from his city years -- sits underneath the renovation stall, unconnected in his
  own mind.
- PLAYER INTERVENTION: repeated gentle presence over many visits (not one clever line) is what
  actually earns the deflection cracking; pushing hard early only repeats the deflection.
- DELAY: `RELATIONSHIP_EVIDENCE` requires *specific* earned actions (see ledger), not a raw
  conversation-count variable -- this is the direct, real-derived-grounded fix for the exact gap the
  V1 playthrough hit (Daisuke's card thread never fired despite many visits, because only free-talk
  moved the old counter).
- CALLBACK: the renovation decision (E23) and the card reveal (E20) are structurally the same
  pattern shown twice, at two different sizes -- never named as parallel in-fiction, felt only by an
  attentive player.
- PAYOFF: decides on the renovation mid-game (a deliberately shorter arc than the others -- not
  every thread needs to wait for Day 30), and may or may not answer the card by Day 30, independent
  of the renovation outcome.

## Jin, 57 — independent handyman [episodes ORIGINAL_FICTION]

- SEED: wants enough work to stay genuinely needed; secretly would take a steadier arrangement if
  offered right.
- CONTRADICTION: shows care entirely through unasked-for competence, never through being told he's
  needed -- and would never ask for the steadier arrangement himself even though he wants it.
- COMPLICATION: is often the least-thanked person in town for work everyone benefits from, not from
  bitterness, just because he never asks to be noticed -- and a private, never-voiced-directly worry
  about whether working alone forever is really sustainable sits under the dry jokes.
- PLAYER INTERVENTION: specifically noticing and naming his usually-invisible work (not just
  visiting him) is what generates real relationship evidence -- mere presence does not.
- DELAY: the standing-arrangement offer requires real accumulated evidence (specific noticed/thanked
  moments), not a raw visit count -- and, per the V1 verdict's open question, this rebuild explicitly
  allows a SECOND, later opportunity to surface it if missed early, rather than permanently locking
  an entire arc behind a Day-5-only window.
- CALLBACK: his own unvoiced worry (never directly askable) softens only as a felt texture change in
  his dialogue register over time if the arrangement was accepted -- never stated outright.
- PAYOFF: an ongoing quiet companionship if accepted; if declined or never reached, he stays exactly
  himself, no worse for it, no judgment attached.

## Yohei, 63 — general store owner [E09 REAL_OWNER_DERIVED (business-pressure texture only, not the
son-thread), other episodes ORIGINAL_FICTION]

- SEED: wants the shop running smoothly, especially around the festival; underneath that, is afraid
  he's already lost his son over never reaching out.
- CONTRADICTION: kind and generous with strangers in small, practical ways; flatly unable to reach
  out to his own son over something much smaller than a stranger's problem. Treats every newcomer
  with the same reflexive wariness he showed Hina and doesn't register it as anything but "being
  careful."
- COMPLICATION: an unopened message from his son sits unaddressed for over a year -- the gap wasn't
  a decision either side made, just something that happened by not-acting.
- PLAYER INTERVENTION: noticing the phone-glance moment and asking gently (deep-talk only) is what
  creates the relationship evidence that gates the eventual payoff -- never forceable, never a single
  "correct" phrase.
- DELAY: real time must pass and real evidence must accumulate between the noticing (early-mid game)
  and the son actually calling (late game) -- a single day's conversation cannot compress this.
- CALLBACK: the "unusually quiet morning" opening of the payoff day directly echoes the original
  "glances at his phone, puts it away fast" noticing moment -- same gesture, different meaning by
  then.
- PAYOFF: the son calls; nothing is fully resolved, they simply talked -- deliberately not a full
  reunion, gated on the earlier noticing having actually happened.

## Miyoko, 68 — café owner [episodes ORIGINAL_FICTION]

- SEED: wants the café to still feel like somewhere people actually want to sit; her daughter wants
  her to close it and move closer.
- CONTRADICTION: endlessly patient and unhurried with every customer; privately anxious and
  rehearsing before something as small as serving a new coffee bean. Warm to everyone equally by
  design (so no one reads early kindness as special treatment) -- which also means it's easy to miss
  when she needs something back.
- COMPLICATION: doesn't actually know yet what she'll tell her daughter, and not-knowing scares her
  more than either answer would.
- PLAYER INTERVENTION: listening without pushing toward either outcome is the correct-shaped
  intervention here -- this arc explicitly should not be "solvable" by the player choosing a side.
- DELAY: this is the slowest, most 30-day-scaled arc of the six by design -- real evidence
  (repeated presence during her more anxious/quiet days, not just cheerful ones) accumulates across
  nearly the whole month before the daughter-thread reaches any real beat.
- CALLBACK: how confidently or anxiously the new-beans moment (early game) landed colors the emotional
  register of the daughter-thread's resolution (late game) -- a felt throughline, not a stated one.
- PAYOFF: staying, a compromise, or genuinely still undecided by Day 30 -- all three explicitly
  legitimate, unscored, weighted by real accumulated evidence, never a coin flip.

## Fumiko, 70 — retired teacher, community hall [episodes ORIGINAL_FICTION]

- SEED: wants the hall to stay a place people actually drop into; fears that retirement has quietly
  made her less needed, and that she's lonelier for it than her brisk manner admits.
- CONTRADICTION: refuses to simply replace the hall's wobbly bench, which reads as stubbornness but
  is actually quiet, unstated sentimental attachment. Her direct, blunt manner is often misread as
  short-temperedness, and she doesn't notice how often this happens.
- COMPLICATION: the bench was built by a former student she's lost touch with, who unexpectedly
  writes to her -- old regret surfacing without warning.
- PLAYER INTERVENTION: connecting her to Jin (for the bench) is a real, structural piece of evidence;
  simply asking about the letter is a separate, deep-talk-gated piece of evidence -- the two are
  related but independently earned.
- DELAY: the letter's arrival and the bench's actual repair can happen on different days
  entirely, and the strongest emotional payoff requires BOTH pieces of evidence to exist before the
  festival, not either alone.
- CALLBACK: the festival day's public use of the fixed bench is a direct visual callback to the
  private letter moment, without ever stating the connection in-fiction.
- PAYOFF: the bench gets fixed if connected to Jin; the letter thread can resolve into her writing
  back by Day 30, or simply sit un-answered-or-not -- equally legitimate either way.
