# NEW LIFE — 30-Day First Draft V1

> **SUPERSEDED, KEPT AS REFERENCE ONLY.** Not implemented, not binding. `NEW_LIFE_CORE_REDESIGN_V1`
> replaced this Run's premise before Day1 vertical-slice work began. The single-throughline,
> career-destination-driven shape below is exactly what the new directive says NOT to build
> (outcomes must accumulate from free NPC interaction, never be pre-authored toward one path).
> Individual scene textures (rain/20-minute wait, the supermarket non-event, Miyoko's misheard
> gossip about Kamiya paid off on Day16) remain useful examples of the "meaningful waste" and
> "planted detail pays off" discipline the new directive also wants, and were drawn on loosely
> when writing DAY1's world-event seeds.

One representative path, written whole, the way a novel's first draft gets written before
branches are designed. This is the story of a protagonist who ends up, by Day 30, staying in
Challenge Town, growing into a real (if not yet legally formal) partnership in Yohei's store, and
heading — visibly, not yet married — toward a life with Maki, helping her get the flower shop's
books and ordering onto a system that actually works. That is ONE path. The Story Bible's other
14 careers and 5 life-statuses are not lesser; this is simply the one thread pulled all the way
through, per directive, before any branching gets designed.

Not every day gets full scene treatment. Per directive: some days are two or three lines of real,
specific texture, not "nothing happened" placeholder. Days that matter get more.

---

## DAY 1

Morning: he wakes in the trial house before he means to, too early, the way you do the first
night somewhere new. The room came with a folder — keys, a laminated map, a return date circled
in red pen by someone at the Center. He reads it twice, standing up, still in yesterday's shirt.

He walks to the Challenge Center because the folder told him to. Kamiya's office is small,
handled — a desk that's clearly seen a lot of first meetings. Kamiya runs through the standard
opening (30 days, come by anytime, here's who does what) at a pace that says he's done this
dozens of times, and then, closing the folder, asks the actual question: *"一つだけ聞かせてくだ
さい。あなた、本当は何がしたいんです?"* Free text. Whatever the protagonist types, Kamiya's
actual spoken reply stays close to neutral — he's heard versions of most answers — but the
narration allows one beat: Kamiya writes something down that isn't quite what was said. Not
wrong, exactly. A category.

Afternoon: he walks the shopping street for the first time without a task. Finds Yohei's store
first, drawn in by nothing more than an open door and a place to stand out of the sun. Yohei
is mid-sentence to no one in particular, hauling a box of the festival stock in from the back.
*"棚の奥から、こんなものが出てきてな。"* An old toolbox, dust on the hinges. *"まあ、まだ使え
るんだけどな。"* That's all he says about it — doesn't explain why he's showing it to a stranger,
doesn't ask if he wants it. The protagonist, who spent thirty years being the person who asks
follow-up questions, asks one anyway: can he borrow it? Yohei's surprised enough to actually look
at him for a second before saying yes.

He finds Miyoko's café by smell before sign. She's wiping a cup that's already clean, delighted
by nothing more than a face she doesn't recognize. Talks fast, a little too much, about the
weather, about a regular who hasn't been in for a week, about nothing in particular — and he lets
it wash over him, doesn't try to steer it anywhere, which she seems to appreciate more than she'd
say.

Evening: walking back, he passes a storefront with its shutter half down and a hand-lettered
notice taped inside the glass — *「近日、何か始めます」* — no name, no date. He doesn't ask
anyone about it. Just remembers it's there.

Night: he unpacks exactly the things he needs for tomorrow and leaves the rest in the bag. Doesn't
call his daughter yet — decides that's a tomorrow thing. Sits with the toolbox on the floor for a
minute before going to bed, not doing anything with it. Just looking at it.

---

## DAY 2

Morning: at the community hall — nothing urgent, he just wanted to see the rest of the five real
places in this town before deciding which ones matter — he finds Jin taking down an old notice
from the board. Jin clocks him without much interest. *"働いてないの?"* Not really a question,
more a category he's placing him in. *"今は。"* *"へえ。"* A beat. Then Jin reaches into a bag,
pulls out a spare pair of work gloves, holds them out without ceremony. *"これ持って。午後、現場
について来るか。"*

He says yes before he's decided whether it's a good idea.

Afternoon: the "現場" turns out to be three separate small jobs across town — a hinge, a leaking
tap, moving something heavy that two people can move and one can't. Jin doesn't explain any of it
in advance. Hands him things, says short instructions ("持って", "そっち"), expects him to keep
up. He does, mostly. At one point Jin asks, out of nowhere, walking between jobs, *"にしても、何
でこの町に来たんだ?"* — free text. Whatever's said, Jin doesn't push on it, just files it the way
he files everything, and keeps walking.

Evening: he's tired in a way he hasn't been in years — the specific tiredness of using muscles
that spend most days sitting in meetings. Doesn't mind it. Skips dinner out, eats something simple
at the trial house, asleep early.

---

## DAY 3

Morning: stiff. Notices it, doesn't complain to anyone about it. Walks to the café for coffee
mostly because sitting down sounds good. Miyoko's alone, visibly behind — a delivery came in
wrong, someone who usually helps on Saturdays didn't show. *"今日、ちょっと人が足りなくて。"* Not
a request. He offers anyway. She's surprised enough to actually say yes.

They work through the lunch rush together — him bussing tables and running the register badly,
her doing everything else while narrating half of it. Somewhere in the middle of it, waiting on a
pot to finish brewing with nothing to do for a few minutes, she tells him — unprompted, slightly
wrong, he'll find out later — that Kamiya "used to be married, I heard," which is not quite what
happened (he was never married; the relationship that ended wasn't a marriage) but close enough
in shape that the protagonist files it as fact and it'll cost him a small, real awkward moment
with Kamiya eventually, not today.

Afternoon: rain starts, unscheduled, the kind that changes a day's shape without announcing it.
He'd meant to walk out to the far edge of town, just to see it; doesn't. Sits under the café's
awning for twenty minutes with nothing happening, waiting for it to ease, watching the street.
Nothing in this twenty minutes advances any thread. It is, on purpose, dead time — the kind the
directive explicitly wants and the kind most drafts of this game have been too nervous to include.

Evening: back at Yohei's, briefly, to return nothing in particular — just passing. Yohei's
closing up. *"相馬の仕事、手伝ったんだって?"* — small towns. *"だろうな。"* That's the whole
conversation. It's enough.

---

## DAY 4

Yohei's wrestling with the festival order's paperwork — a stack of supplier invoices, half of it
in a handwriting he can barely read himself, no system beyond a shoebox. *"数字、苦手でな。"* The
protagonist, three decades of exactly this kind of work behind him, offers to look. Spends an hour
sorting it into something that actually adds up. Yohei watches part of it, says little, but the
next time someone asks him for a delivery date he answers without digging through the shoebox —
a small, real, visible effect, not commented on by either of them.

Free text, later, at the café: Miyoko, refilling his cup without being asked, *"前は何してたの?"*
Whatever he answers, she nods like she already half-knew, because of course she's heard some
version from someone.

Evening: he calls his daughter. Short call. She asks how it's going; he says "fine, settling in,"
which is true and also not the whole of it. She doesn't push. Neither says anything about her
mother. That's the whole scene.

---

## DAY 5

He goes to the Center for what Kamiya's folder called a "5日目の面談" — sounds more formal than
it turns out to be. Kamiya's not there; out on a site visit that ran long. Another staffer,
younger, covers — pleasant, generic, has clearly not been briefed on the protagonist specifically,
treats him like a category rather than a case. It's a small, deliberately unglamorous scene: the
Center is not a stage built around him. It has its own schedule, and today he's not the main
event.

He leaves a note. Kamiya calls him that evening instead — the first time they've spoken outside
the office. Short, a little apologetic, mostly just: *"どうでした、最初の一週間。"* Free text.
Whatever's said, Kamiya's reply carries a private note of relief the protagonist can't read — the
week's activity (Yohei's shop, Jin's job, Miyoko's counter) doesn't match the pattern Kamiya filed
him under on Day 1, and Kamiya notices that without saying so.

---

## DAY 6

Shopping street, midday: he sees the shuttered storefront again — someone's inside now, a shape
moving behind the glass, a stack of flat-packed shelving leaned against the wall outside. He
doesn't go in. Just registers: something's actually starting.

At the supermarket that evening, buying nothing important, he sees the same staffer from the
Center's counter yesterday, off duty, buying dinner for one, and they exchange the kind of nod two
people give each other when they've technically met once. Nothing else happens. This is
deliberately a non-event — the town recognizing him back, in the smallest possible way.

## DAY 7

Community hall roof is leaking; Jin's up on a ladder about it, on his own initiative, nobody asked.
The protagonist watches for a while, doesn't insert himself — this isn't his to help with today,
and some part of him is starting to recognize the difference between "useful" and "wanted." Jin
doesn't ask him up. That's fine. He walks on.

Evening: writes, for the first time, an actual private note to himself — not the spreadsheet kind,
just a line — "洋平の店の帳面、まだ気になる。" One sentence. Closes the notebook.

---

## DAY 8

Yohei, closing the shop, sorting stock nobody's watching him sort: *"この店、継ぐ奴がいないんだ
よな。"* Said to the air more than to the protagonist. The protagonist could ask more. Doesn't,
this time — three decades of reading rooms tells him this isn't the moment, Yohei said it to get
it out, not to open it. He just says *"そうですか,"* and lets it sit. (This restraint matters —
Day 17 is where he chooses differently.)

## DAY 9

Miyoko's daughter calls while he happens to be at the café. He only hears Miyoko's side — the
warmth going a little flat, the *"また今度考える"* she's clearly said many times before. After she
hangs up she doesn't explain it, just pours two coffees instead of one and sits down across from
him for five minutes, saying nothing about it. He doesn't ask. This is the daughter thread's first
real appearance — not explained, just witnessed.

## DAY 10

A quiet day, mostly his own: laundry, a long walk out past the community hall to where the town
thins into rice fields, nothing eventful. He runs into no one who matters. This is deliberate —
not every day needs a scene with a named character in it.

## DAY 11

Second half-day with Jin — not an "offer" this time, Jin just says *"来るか"* like it's already
assumed. Different job (a fence, this time), same terse instruction style, but the protagonist
keeps up better. Jin, packing up, almost as an aside: *"筋、悪くないな。"* Doesn't elaborate. It's
the most Jin has praised anyone all week, by Jin's standards.

## DAY 12

The storefront's shutter is fully up for the first time. A woman he doesn't recognize is arguing,
good-naturedly, with a delivery driver about where to put a pallet of terracotta pots. Jin's there
too, tool bag open, working on the door frame — this is how the protagonist actually meets Maki:
introduced, in effect, by Jin, mid-argument, over pots. *"新しい人?"* she asks Jin, not the
protagonist, without breaking focus on the pallet. Jin: *"知らん。"* First exchange between the
protagonist and Maki is three sentences and entirely about where the pots should not go.

## DAY 13

He comes back the next day without a clear reason to, tells himself it's because he's curious
about the shop, helps carry shelving for twenty minutes. Maki works fast, decides things fast,
changes her mind twice in the time it takes him to screw in four brackets, and narrates none of
it as indecision — just as "this way's better, forget the last way." He notices he wants to ask
her to slow down and doesn't.

## DAY 14

Shared task, real stakes for her, not staged: the flower delivery for opening week is arriving in
two days and her ordering sheet is a mess of crossed-out numbers. He offers to sort it — the same
offer he made Yohei on Day 4, but this time she says, before he's finished the sentence, *"うん、
お願い。"* No hesitation, no surprise at being helped, unlike Yohei — a real character difference,
not restated as one. He spends two hours on it. She thanks him once, briefly, moves on to the next
thing before he's finished being thanked. This is who she is.

---

## DAY 15 — Midpoint

Evening, trial house. He sits down to actually do the thing he'd planned to do all along —
write out, properly, a comparison: the factory lead Kamiya mentioned once, the DX-support idea
that's been circling since the festival paperwork, Yohei's shop, staying, going. Three columns,
the way he built a hundred decision memos in his old job. Gets two lines in and stops, because
the columns don't hold what's actually pulling at him — Yohei's silence about his son isn't a
data point, neither is the specific way Maki said "うん、お願い" without hesitating. He sits with
the half-written page for a while, doesn't finish it, doesn't throw it away either. Closes the
notebook on it.

No one tells him this is what's happening. It just is.

Separately, same week: Kamiya, reviewing his own casual notes ahead of a supervisor check-in,
finds his own Day-1 category-note on the protagonist and has to cross it out — the pattern hasn't
held. He doesn't say this to the protagonist. It shows only in how the next conversation between
them goes: Kamiya asks a genuinely open question instead of a diagnostic one, for the first time.

---

## DAY 16

Community hall, incidental: he runs into the Day-5 staffer again, chats for real this time — five
minutes, nothing plot-relevant, just two people who now recognize each other. The town keeps
accumulating small, unremarkable familiarity like this.

Later, at the Center, a small, real awkwardness: something in the conversation with Kamiya makes
the protagonist reference, glancingly, "your marriage" — not as a question, just in passing, the
way you'd refer to something you'd been told as settled fact. Kamiya's face does something brief
and hard to name before he corrects it, mildly: *"結婚はしてないですよ。……付き合ってた人はいま
したけど。"* Doesn't explain further, doesn't seem angry, just faintly closed for the rest of the
conversation. The protagonist doesn't apologize elaborately — just a plain *"すみません、勘違い
してました"* — and neither of them brings it up again. This is Day 3's café gossip actually
costing something, on screen, the way a planted detail should.

## DAY 17

He goes back to Yohei's specifically to not let the Day-8 line drop. Doesn't announce it as a
serious conversation — just stays in the store past the point where Yohei would usually find a
reason to send him off, and when Yohei tries the subject-change (*"それより、明日の配送が—"*), he
doesn't take the exit. *"息子さんの話、ですけど。"* Yohei goes quiet for a second — genuinely
caught, not performed — then actually talks, briefly, plainly, about calling his son and not
knowing what he'd even say. This is the choice point the Story Bible names: the protagonist chose
to be the one who didn't let the subject change. It could have gone the other way.

## DAY 18

Value clash with Maki, overdue: he raises, carefully, that she might want to slow the opening
timeline down — a supplier issue that a few more days would fix cleanly, that she's planning to
push through anyway. She hears it as him not trusting her judgment; he hears her dismissal as
recklessness. Neither is entirely right. It's a real argument, short, not resolved by the end of
the scene — she opens on the original date, slightly worse for it, and doesn't apologize, and
he doesn't either, and the tension sits unresolved for several days.

## DAY 19

Distance, by circumstance: the flower shop's opening week eats all of Maki's time and most of
Jin's; the protagonist isn't part of either and spends the day at loose ends, doing his own
laundry, reading the return-the-key folder again without meaning to, noticing the date. This is
the required "goes quiet" stretch — nothing wrong happened, the thread just isn't being tended,
which is the point.

---

## DAY 20

Kamiya's follow-up call: the DX-support connection he'd floated weeks ago — a real business (not
named further, kept plausible-and-small: a local company needing help digitizing scheduling and
inventory) — has an actual opening, and an actual deadline: two weeks to accept the intro meeting
or it goes to someone else in the program. First real external clock.

## DAY 21

He goes and looks at the shop's books properly for the first time — Maki's asked him to, post-
argument, in the driest possible way ("直して" with a shrug, no apology attached and none
expected) — and it's bad: a system that will cost her real money within a season if it's not
fixed. This is the moment the SME_AI_DX_SUPPORT thread stops being incidental and becomes
something he might actually build.

## DAY 22 — Schedule collision

Two things land on the same day: the Kamiya-arranged intro meeting for the DX-support opportunity
(external, in the next town over, a real commute) and the festival day at Yohei's shop, which
needs every hand it can get and which Yohei, still not fully past his own avoidance, hasn't
directly asked for help with — the protagonist knows anyway. He can't do both. He picks the
festival. Calls to reschedule the meeting; Kamiya, professionally, says that's not really how
these work — the business is choosing between candidates this month. The protagonist says he
understands. This is the day the "全部できる" door actually closes on one specific opportunity.

## DAY 23

Festival day. Full scene, minor: Yohei running the stall, the protagonist actually working it
alongside him without being asked twice, Jin dropping by between jobs to eat something off a
stick and needle Yohei about the prices, Miyoko closing the café early to come watch, Maki
stopping by for ten minutes between shop tasks. All five people in one place for the only time in
the draft, briefly, doing nothing important — this is not a "found family" speech, just a normal
festival day with people who now know each other.

## DAY 24

Quiet aftermath. Yohei, tired, honest in a way festival-day adrenaline makes easier: *"今日は助
かった。"* Nothing more. It's the closest he's come to naming what's been building.

## DAY 25

Money, concretely, once: he sits down and actually does the math — savings minus 30-plus days,
against what staying and building something slow with Yohei's shop (or Maki's books) would
realistically pay in the near term versus what the now-closed DX opportunity would have paid
immediately. It's not comfortable. He doesn't resolve it today. Just does the arithmetic and
writes down a number.

---

## DAY 26

He tells Maki, plainly, that he turned down the DX-support meeting for the festival, and that he'd
still like to help fix her shop's books properly, on a real, ongoing basis, not as a favor. She's
quiet for a second — this is new, an offer with no exit built into it — then says, characteristically
fast once she's decided, *"じゃあ、お願いします"* — the second time she's said almost exactly the
same three words as Day 14, but they carry very differently now.

## DAY 27

Yohei, unprompted this time, no subject-change: *"店のこと、真面目に考えるか。"* Not yet a formal
offer of succession — just the first time Yohei has raised it himself, without being asked twice.
The protagonist says yes, he'd like to. Nothing is signed. That's not how this resolves in 30 days,
and shouldn't be.

## DAY 28

He and Maki, closing the shop together, quiet: the pace/permanence argument from Day 18 comes back
up, briefly, on its own — not re-litigated, just acknowledged. She admits, once, that she's scared
of doing the slow thing and failing at it the way the fast things eventually failed. He doesn't
fix this for her. Just stays in the room while she says it.

## DAY 29

Last full ordinary day. He calls his daughter again — longer this time, tells her, for the first
time, that he might not come back, might actually stay. She's quiet, then glad, in the plain,
undramatic way that call would actually go. Still no mention of her mother. That's still not
this story's job to force open.

---

## DAY 30

Morning, ordinary: he wakes at a normal hour, not before it, for maybe the first time since Day 1.
Walks the same route he's walked most mornings for weeks. Yohei's setting up the store, same as
always, not staged as a goodbye. Miyoko's café is open, same as always. Jin's not around — there's
a job somewhere across town, and that's fine, that's just Tuesday for him. Kamiya, per the
original folder, has a closing meeting scheduled; keeps it.

At the Center: Kamiya doesn't ask a diagnostic question this time. Just: *"それで、どうします?"*
Free text, the last one. The protagonist answers with whatever the player actually built across
30 days — in this draft's throughline: staying, working toward taking on real responsibility at
Yohei's shop, building out the bookkeeping/ordering work with Maki's shop as something closer to
a second, chosen occupation, the two threads not merged into one tidy business but both real,
both his. Life-status: 町に残る, heading toward something with Maki that is visibly serious and
explicitly not yet a wedding.

He returns the key that afternoon. The folder said to. It's a small, physical, unglamorous beat —
handing over a key — and the draft ends there, not on a summary of lessons learned, not on a
score. Just a man who has somewhere to be tomorrow, in this town, and knows it now in a way he
didn't on Day 1.
