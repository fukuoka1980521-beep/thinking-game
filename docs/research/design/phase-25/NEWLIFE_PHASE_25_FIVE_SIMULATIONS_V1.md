# NEW LIFE — Five World Simulations V1 (PHASE_25, Section 13)

Simulated against `NEWLIFE_THREE_EVENT_ENGINE_V1.md` and `NEWLIFE_EVENT_NPC_MATRIX_V1.md`, using
the transition rule and non-linear discipline from `NEWLIFE_CHARACTER_TRANSFORMATION_ENGINE_V1.md`.
No 30-day story is written yet — these are event-level simulations only, per Section 13's own scope.
Starting `CHARACTER_STATE` before Event 1 is identical across all five runs (each NPC at their
baseline needs/traits from `NEWLIFE_CHARACTER_MODELS_V3.md`); only the PLAYER persona differs.

## RUN_1 — PLAYER highly social

**Persona**: talks to everyone, often, warmly — breadth over depth. Attends every scene available,
rarely misses a conversation, rarely asks a hard or specific question.

**Event 1**: broad, low-friction goodwill across all six — everyone gets a warm word from the
player. The player does NOT specifically cross-check Fumiko's plan against Jin's build (too spread
thin across six relationships to notice one specific technical mismatch) — the Event 2 setup
proceeds unchanged. Hina gets genuine company, which softens her Event-1 anxiety slightly
(`needs_state.relatedness.satisfaction` ticks to MEDIUM instead of staying LOW).

**Event 2**: the wiring problem occurs exactly as designed (a WORLD fact, not player-preventable).
The player is present and comforting to several people at once — Hina, Miyoko — but this breadth
means no one gets the *specific* attention their own defense actually needs (Jin needed to be
asked directly, not generally comforted; he stays silent). Regression: Fumiko/Jin tension proceeds
unresolved, same as the no-player baseline.

**Event 3**: broad social warmth has built real but shallow trust everywhere. Daisuke, whose own
transformation responds well to low-pressure warmth without demanding depth, makes his small,
time-bounded commitment (P28) partly because the player's steady presence made the town feel
slightly less risky to commit to. Jin says nothing — no one specifically asked. Fumiko keeps full
control — no one specifically advocated for delegating to Jin. Hina, warmed but not specifically
helped to ask, decides to try again alone.

**Final states**: Daisuke `ATTEMPTED->ADVANCED` (small commitment made). Jin `NONE` (unchanged).
Yohei `NONE` (never confronted or asked to own the equipment choice). Miyoko `NONE` (never asked
what she wants). Fumiko `NONE` (never asked to delegate). Hina `ATTEMPTED` (asks no one, but is
visibly less isolated than baseline).

**Which changed / why**: only Daisuke, and only because his specific transformation happens to
respond to broad, low-pressure warmth rather than a targeted question — everyone else's axis
specifically requires depth or a direct ask this breadth-first persona never provides.

## RUN_2 — PLAYER practical / helpful / respects privacy

**Persona**: shows up to do concrete tasks, asks practical questions, never pries into feelings.

**Event 1**: this persona's practical questions are exactly the kind that could surface the Fumiko/
Jin plan mismatch — the player, helping physically, notices the build doesn't match the posted plan
and mentions it to Jin directly (a practical fact, not an emotional check-in, which fits both the
player's style and Jin's own preference for plain fact over sympathy). Jin adjusts before the day of;
Fumiko never learns her own plan was off, since the fix happens at Jin's end without the player
routing it through her.

**Event 2**: because the mismatch was partly caught, the wiring failure still occurs (system-owned,
not preventable — the secondhand equipment itself was the deeper cause, untouched by this fix) but
its narrated severity is slightly lower (a smaller spark, no burn) since the setup around it was
tidier. The player helps with cleanup efficiently, checks on Jin's hand practically ("let me see
that") rather than emotionally — this actually lands well with Jin specifically (matches his own
action-not-words love-language) even though the player never asks how he "feels."

**Event 3**: the player's practical, factual question to Yohei about the equipment's sourcing
directly surfaces his role, without confrontation — Yohei, responding to plain fact rather than
emotional pressure (per his own speech model), owns it more readily than in the no-player baseline.
This measurably shifts Event 3 toward the "real, regular fixture with redistributed responsibility"
branch. Jin, however, still never states his own want directly — the player's privacy-respecting
style never asks him the one direct personal question his axis actually requires.

**Final states**: Yohei `ATTEMPTED->ADVANCED` (owns the equipment choice). Fumiko `NONE`
(never asked to delegate, though Jin's real competence was quietly demonstrated, which may color a
*future* month, not scored here). Jin `NONE` (helped practically, never asked to voice a want).
Hina `NONE->ATTEMPTED` if the player happens to help her prep in Event 1 (real, concrete help fits
this persona) but she still doesn't proactively ask for help in Event 3 unless it's offered first.
Daisuke, Miyoko: `NONE` (this persona doesn't target either's specific need).

**Which changed / why**: Yohei, uniquely — this persona's practical, non-confrontational fact-
checking is exactly what his DEFENSE->REALITY ACCEPTANCE axis responds to (plain fact, not
emotional appeal), a genuine divergence from RUN_1 where he never moved at all.

## RUN_3 — PLAYER blunt / confrontational

**Persona**: direct, sometimes sharp, asks hard questions plainly, doesn't soften delivery.

**Event 1**: bluntly asks Fumiko whether her plan actually matches what Jin's building — a direct,
almost confrontational framing. Fumiko (HIGH Agency, MEDIUM Agreeableness) does NOT respond well to
being challenged this early — she becomes MORE controlling in the short term (a REGRESSION before
Event 2 even happens), doubling down rather than checking. The mismatch proceeds into Event 2
unchanged, now with added tension between Fumiko and the player specifically.

**Event 2**: the player directly asks Jin, "what did you actually think would happen with that
wiring?" — blunt, but exactly the kind of plain, direct question Jin's own voice model responds to
far better than a soft "are you okay." Jin, unusually, gives a real, if short, factual answer: yes,
he'd wondered about the equipment. This is `ATTEMPTED` progress on his OTHER-FOCUS->OWN VOICE axis
— triggered by bluntness, not warmth, a genuine and specific divergence from every other run.
Separately, the same bluntness applied to Hina (asking her directly why she's shutting down) lands
badly — her HIGH Neuroticism and CORE fear of being "seen and found lacking" makes direct
confrontation read as exactly the judgment she was afraid of; she withdraws further, a real
regression distinct from the baseline.

**Event 3**: the player's blunt challenge to Yohei about the equipment gets a defensive, minimizing
answer initially (he doesn't respond to confrontation the way he responds to plain fact delivered
without an edge, per RUN_2's contrast) — no ownership this run. But the player's earlier bluntness
with Jin pays off further: Jin, having already said one true thing in Event 2, states his actual
need in Event 3 when asked again just as directly — `ADVANCED`. Fumiko, still cool toward the
player from Event 1, does not delegate to Jin despite his readiness — a real, frustrating,
legitimate outcome where two characters' own axes are ready but the specific bridge between them
doesn't form this month.

**Final states**: Jin `ATTEMPTED->ADVANCED` (uniquely realized furthest in this run). Fumiko `NONE`
(actively cooler than baseline due to early confrontation). Hina `NONE`, worse than baseline (a real
regression). Yohei `NONE`. Daisuke, Miyoko: `NONE` (untargeted this run).

**Which changed / why**: Jin moved furthest of any run so far, specifically because bluntness
matches his own plain-fact speech model; Hina moved backward, specifically because the same
bluntness violates her own sensitivity — the clearest demonstration yet that no single player style
is universally good or bad, per Section 13's own question ("did the five runs genuinely diverge?").

## RUN_4 — PLAYER passive / observes

**Persona**: present, attentive, intervenes in almost nothing. Watches scenes unfold without acting.

**Event 1**: no player action anywhere in the matrix's PLAYER INTERVENTION OPPORTUNITY column fires.
Every NO-PLAYER OUTCOME occurs exactly as authored: Fumiko's plan and Jin's build proceed
unchecked toward their mismatch.

**Event 2**: the wiring problem occurs exactly as designed. Every NPC's own default reaction fires
unmodified: Hina catastrophizes and recovers alone after a day or two; Daisuke's humor smooths the
group mood at the cost of a real debrief; Jin absorbs guilt silently; Yohei minimizes; Miyoko
over-apologizes and gives away free coffee; Fumiko overcorrects into unilateral new rules. All
NO-PLAYER OUTCOMES, none interrupted.

**Event 3**: the town decision proceeds on accumulated NPC-only state. Given the Event-1/2 chain
(unresolved Fumiko/Jin tension, no one having surfaced the equipment's real cause, no one having
directly asked Jin anything), the most probable branch is (b) from the Three Event Engine's list —
the market day happens again once, informally, with the same unresolved ambiguity, since no single
NPC's own axis reliably self-resolves without an external prompt (this matches each character
model's own NON-TRANSFORMATION POSSIBILITY field, which is explicitly designed to be the DEFAULT
absent intervention, not a rare edge case).

**Final states**: all six at `NONE`, and explicitly, per each character model's own NON-
TRANSFORMATION POSSIBILITY, this is narrated as complete, dignified, and real, not empty. Hina still
opens or doesn't open the stall business on her own separate schedule; Daisuke's two matters still
resolve on their own independent clocks; Jin remains exactly himself; Yohei's business relationships
remain exactly as competent and gruff as ever; Miyoko keeps deferring; Fumiko keeps everything
herself and the Hall keeps running.

**Which changed / why**: none. This is the explicit, required proof (Section 12) that a fully
passive player still receives a complete, coherent, non-empty world — the world's own NPC-NPC
motion (Yohei/Daisuke's friendship, Miyoko/Fumiko's, Jin's ongoing repairs) continues to be
genuinely witnessable even though no character's private axis moves.

## RUN_5 — PLAYER inconsistent / sometimes socially harmful

**Persona**: kind in some scenes, careless or actively unhelpful in others — forgets a stated
promise, laughs at an inopportune moment, occasionally undermines someone without meaning real harm.

**Event 1**: warmly helps Hina rehearse (a real, positive beat, `hina_evidence` gains an item) but
separately promises Miyoko he'll "definitely" come to the stall's opening and then doesn't show —
a small, real broken commitment. Miyoko (HIGH Agreeableness, low agency) doesn't confront this, but
privately logs it (a real `memories[]` entry, not forgotten).

**Event 2**: during the immediate aftermath of the spark, the player laughs — nervously, not
maliciously — at Jin fumbling to unplug the equipment. This lands badly and specifically: Jin's
LOW-Extraversion, HIGH-Conscientiousness profile reads this as being laughed at for a real, if minor,
injury and mistake — a genuine, specific regression in trust with the player alone (not with other
NPCs, since they didn't witness it). Separately, the player DOES sit with Hina during her spiral
without trying to fix it (a real, positive, correctly-shaped intervention per Section 12's own
"neighbor, not therapist" model) — `hina_evidence` gains real weight here, a genuine positive
counterweight in the same run.

**Event 3**: mixed, divergent results by relationship, not a uniform verdict on the player. Yohei,
whose baseline trust in anyone is already LOW-MEDIUM and who doesn't hold newcomers to a high bar
(he's gruff with everyone equally), is largely unaffected by the inconsistency — his own relationship
with the player stays roughly at baseline. Miyoko, however, having privately logged the broken
promise, is noticeably cooler and more careful with the player specifically for the rest of the
month, without ever saying so directly (consistent with her own conflict-avoidant speech model —
she doesn't confront, she just quietly recalibrates trust). Jin remains guarded with the player
specifically (not with the town generally) following the laughing incident — his own transformation
axis stays `NONE` with respect to this player, though the model would allow it to fire with a
DIFFERENT, more careful player persona, or later in the month if directly, sincerely addressed.

**Final states**: Hina `ATTEMPTED` (the one real positive thread this run, via the Event 2
sitting-with-her beat). Miyoko `NONE`, cooler specifically toward the player (a real, earned
consequence, not scored punitively — just consistently applied). Jin `NONE`, guarded specifically
toward the player. Yohei, Daisuke, Fumiko: `NONE`, roughly at baseline (their own specific axes were
never targeted this run, harmfully or otherwise).

**Which changed / why**: only Hina moved forward, and specifically because of one correctly-shaped
intervention that happened to land amid an otherwise inconsistent run — demonstrating that
inconsistency doesn't uniformly damage everyone equally: characters with LOW baseline expectations
of others (Yohei) are more resilient to it than characters who track relational reliability closely
(Miyoko, Jin) — itself a direct, designed consequence of each character's own Agreeableness/
Conscientiousness/Agency profile, not an authored plot punishment.
