# NEW LIFE — Three Event Engine V1 (PHASE_25, Section 6)

Redesigned freely per Section 6's own permission ("redesign freely if a stronger event emerges").
The old design's Days-1-14 solo Hina trial is replaced by a genuine six-person joint project, so
that Event 1 stresses everyone, not just Hina, from the start — directly answering the Owner
playtest's complaint that most NPCs lacked real pressure or individuality.

---

## EVENT 1 — THE PREVIEW STALL (test / small joint project)

**What it is**: the town lets Hina set up a small preview stall *inside the community Hall* during
an existing regular market day (not the full festival) — a genuine six-person joint effort, not a
solo trial the others merely comment on.

**Who contributes what** (this is the mechanism that touches all six differently):
- **Fumiko** grants the Hall space — a real use of her authority, and a real test of her CONTROL
  defense (does she hover and micromanage the space, or actually hand it over for the day?).
- **Yohei** advises on/supplies stock logistics — his COMPETENCE domain, under real pressure (a
  smaller-scale rehearsal of his existing festival-stock decision).
- **Jin** builds/fixes the physical stall structure — his COMPETENCE domain, and a first real
  opportunity for someone to notice (or not notice) his unseen work in a concentrated, visible way.
- **Miyoko** provides coffee to serve alongside it — low-risk for her professionally, but a real
  test of whether she can show up as herself (state a preference, take a bit of the spotlight)
  rather than only in service of others.
- **Daisuke** offers his shopfront as overflow seating — a small, low-stakes version of "opening
  something of his own up," directly adjacent to his AVOIDANCE/DECISION theme without forcing the
  big decision yet.
- **Hina** has to put her actual food in front of real strangers for the first time — her
  COMPETENCE and RELATEDNESS needs both directly on the line.

**WHAT EACH NPC WANTS**: Hina wants proof she isn't repeating the last failure. Fumiko wants the
Hall to feel useful and full again. Yohei wants the stock math to come out clean, painlessly.
Jin wants the structure to simply work, without needing anyone's help mid-day. Miyoko wants to be
liked equally by everyone there (her default mode), with no real personal stake risked. Daisuke
wants to be the easy, helpful friend, without it turning into anything that requires him to decide
something about his own shop.

**WHAT EACH NPC FEARS**: Hina fears public, specific failure (a food item flopping visibly). Fumiko
fears the Hall being treated carelessly, proving she can't actually protect what she's built.
Yohei fears an inventory miscalculation making him look unreliable. Jin fears the structure failing
and it being read as his fault, not a joint planning gap. Miyoko fears being asked to take real
credit or blame for anything, since neutrality is her safety. Daisuke fears being asked to commit
his shopfront regularly, not just once.

**WHAT EACH NPC DOES FIRST** (before player involvement, background-only unless witnessed): Fumiko
draws up a detailed, over-controlling setup plan. Yohei quietly over-orders "just in case," a small
version of his own pressure-cutting pattern. Jin starts the stall structure early, alone, without
asking anyone to confirm the design first (his OTHER-FOCUS pattern — assumes it's not his place to
ask). Miyoko offers to make three different coffee options so no one feels neglected. Daisuke jokes
about the whole thing being "Hina's show" to keep himself comfortably peripheral. Hina rewrites her
short menu four times.

**WHO CLASHES**: Fumiko and Jin — her detailed plan doesn't match what he's already built, and
neither checked with the other first (a direct, concrete collision of CONTROL vs. OTHER-FOCUS/
silent-initiative). **WHO HELPS**: Yohei quietly helps Jin adjust the stock display once he sees the
mismatch (practical competence overriding any friction). **WHO STAYS OUT**: Daisuke, until directly
pulled in — his shopfront is offered but he doesn't proactively check whether it's actually being
used correctly.

**PLAYER INTERVENTION** (optional, never required): can notice the Fumiko/Jin plan mismatch early
and mention it to either side before it becomes visible to everyone; can help Hina rehearse or
simply keep her company; can help physically with the stall; none of this is required for the event
to occur or resolve.

## EVENT 2 — THE WIRING PROBLEM (failure / unexpected problem)

**What it is, and why not a customer complaint**: rather than "the food confused customers" (a
customer-taste problem, already used in the prior design and comparatively low-stakes for anyone but
Hina), Event 2 is a **shared responsibility failure**: the stall's borrowed hot-plate/heating
element — hastily wired in by Jin under the mismatched setup from Event 1, on equipment Yohei
sourced secondhand to save cost (a direct, causal link back to Event 1's own actions, not a random
new accident) — trips and briefly sparks during the market day. No one is seriously hurt (a small,
real scare and a minor burn on Jin's hand from catching it fast), but the Hall's wiring is now a
live safety/liability question, the stall has to shut early, and word spreads immediately that
"something went wrong at the Hall."

**Why this is richer than a taste-confusion problem**: it has more than one plausible cause (Yohei's
secondhand equipment choice, Jin's rushed wiring, Fumiko's Hall/liability oversight, even Hina's own
pressure to get the stall running on time), so blame is genuinely distributed and contestable —
exactly what Section 6's instruction requires ("expose defense, blame, avoidance, leadership, care,
competence, pride, fear... different characters must react differently").

**Reactions, all genuinely different** (background-default, before any player involvement):
- **Yohei** deflects toward pride: "the equipment was fine when I checked it" — minimizes his own
  part (P25/rationalization), privately uneasy but not yet owning it.
- **Fumiko** takes it hardest as a COMPETENCE threat: this is exactly the kind of failure that
  confirms her private fear of being less capable of protecting the Hall than she used to be; she
  overcorrects into rigid, controlling crisis-management (drawing up new rules unilaterally)
  instead of first checking in with anyone.
- **Jin** already half-suspected the equipment before it happened and said nothing (P26/silent
  deference) — now privately carries real, disproportionate guilt, worsened by the literal
  burn, and withdraws further instead of explaining what he actually knew.
- **Daisuke** defuses the group tension with humor almost immediately, which helps in the moment
  and also means no one properly debriefs what happened for at least a day.
- **Miyoko** over-apologizes and over-compensates (free coffee for a week) despite having zero
  actual responsibility for the wiring — her over-helping defense misapplied to a problem that
  isn't hers to fix.
- **Hina** catastrophizes hardest of all — reads the whole incident as proof the venture itself is
  cursed/doomed, a direct echo of her prior shop's collapse, privately spiraling toward
  withdrawing from the whole project rather than continuing.

**Regressions** (Section 11's explicit requirement — some relationships get WORSE before better):
Fumiko and Jin's Event-1 friction sharpens into real, if unspoken, tension — she half-blames his
rushed work, he half-blames her plan forcing the rush, and neither says so directly. Hina pulls back
from Daisuke's easy humor specifically, reading it (unfairly, but understandably under stress) as
not taking the failure seriously.

**PLAYER INTERVENTION** (optional): can help treat/check on Jin directly (a real chance to be the
first person who asks how HE is, not the stall); can specifically ask Yohei or Fumiko a factual
question that surfaces the real cause rather than letting it stay a vague cloud of blame; can sit
with Hina without trying to fix her spiral. None of this is required — the event's facts and the
NPCs' first-order reactions above happen and are witnessable secondhand regardless.

## EVENT 3 — THE MARKET DAY DECISION (collective decision / consequence)

**What it is**: not "the festival happens" — the town must decide, in the days after Event 2,
whether the preview stall becomes a **regular monthly market day** inside the Hall, and if so, who
is actually responsible for what (space, safety/wiring, stock, service, physical setup). This
directly consumes Events 1-2's interpersonal state, per Section 6's explicit requirement
("the final event should consume earlier interpersonal state").

**What's actually being decided, concretely**: (1) does the market day happen again at all; (2) if
yes, does Fumiko delegate real, ongoing responsibility for Hall safety/logistics to someone else
(most naturally Jin, if the Event 2 tension is ever actually resolved) or does she keep it all
herself, unresolved; (3) does Yohei publicly own his equipment choice, or does the record stay
ambiguous; (4) does Jin, for once, say what he actually needs (to be asked, to be consulted on
plans before they're finalized, or simply a fair accounting of what happened) rather than absorbing
it silently again; (5) does Hina commit to trying again at all, and if so, does she ask for real
help planning it this time, or attempt it alone again; (6) does Daisuke commit his shopfront on a
recurring basis (a genuine decision-under-pressure, not a one-off favor), or does he find another
way to stay peripheral; (7) does Miyoko, for once, state an actual preference about her own
continued involvement, rather than agreeing to whatever keeps the peace.

**Multiple legitimate outcomes** (never converges to one ending regardless of state, per Section
14's diversity gate): the market day could (a) become a real, regular fixture with clear, actually
redistributed responsibilities; (b) happen again once, informally, with the same unresolved
ambiguity as before (a realistic, non-catastrophic "nothing really changed" outcome); (c) not
happen again at all, with each character's own specific reason why, none of them villainous; (d) a
mixed outcome — some NPCs commit, others opt out, producing a scaled-down version. Which of these
occurs is read entirely from accumulated Event-1/Event-2 state, never decided by fiat or scored as
"win/lose."

**PLAYER INTERVENTION** (optional, never required): can advocate for a specific responsibility split
in front of the group; can privately encourage one specific NPC toward their own transformation-axis
test (e.g., asking Jin directly what he wants, before the town decision is finalized); can do
nothing and let the NPCs settle it entirely among themselves, which is an explicitly valid, complete
playthrough per Section 12 ("PLAYER IS NOT A THERAPIST").
