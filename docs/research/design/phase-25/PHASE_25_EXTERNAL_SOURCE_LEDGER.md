**G1. McCrae & Costa, "An Introduction to the Five-Factor Model and Its Applications"**
- AUTHORS: Robert R. McCrae, Paul T. Costa Jr. PUBLICATION: *Journal of Personality*, 1992.
- ACCESS: PubMed abstract page (`pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1635039`) blocked by a cookie-consent wall
  this session; claims below rest on WebSearch synthesis of this and related secondary sources
  (ScienceDirect, LibreTexts), not a direct fetch of the paper body.
- SOURCE CLAIM: the Five-Factor Model (Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness,
  Neuroticism — "OCEAN") is the dominant personality-structure framework in the literature,
  validated across natural-language trait adjectives and questionnaire measures, applicable across
  observers and cultures. Neuroticism specifically is described as "negative affectivity and
  emotional instability... the strongest personality predictor of perceived stress."
- LIMITATION: secondary-source synthesis only, no primary-text fetch this session.
- NEW LIFE USE: the five broad traits are used as **qualitative LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH tendencies with a
  behavioral explanation per character** (Section 4's explicit instruction), never as a numeric
  score, never shown to the player, never used as a diagnostic label.

**G2. Wiggins (1979); Leary (1957) — Interpersonal Circumplex**
- SOURCE CLAIM (via `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpersonal_circumplex`, fetched this session): the
  interpersonal circumplex organizes behavior around two orthogonal axes — **Agency/Dominance**
  ("motive and capacity to get ahead," broader than raw dominance, also touching competence and
  activity level) and **Communion/Affiliation** ("motive and capacity to get along," warmth vs.
  coldness/distance). Leary (1957) originated the circle; Wiggins (1979) formalized it
  factor-analytically. The model's own explicit caution (from the fetched page): it is best used as
  "a framework for understanding relational flexibility," not "a rigid diagnostic tool."
- LIMITATION: Wikipedia is a tertiary source; the primary Leary/Wiggins texts were not directly
  fetched this session (a related PDF listing was found but not opened).
- NEW LIFE USE: every NPC gets a qualitative Agency (LOW/MED/HIGH) and Communion (LOW/MED/HIGH)
  rating, used specifically for **disagreement, help-seeking, conflict, closeness, and social
  initiative** behavior (Section 1B's explicit instruction) — never as a fixed personality label
  overriding situational behavior (see G4 below).

**G3. Deci & Ryan — Self-Determination Theory**
- AUTHORS: Edward L. Deci, Richard M. Ryan. PUBLICATION: the theory's own summary page,
  `selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/`, fetched this session; the primary 2000 paper
  ("Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and
  Well-Being," *American Psychologist*) was located but its PDF failed to parse (binary/compression
  artifacts) — claims rest on the theory-summary page, a secondary but author-controlled source.
- SOURCE CLAIM: three basic psychological needs — **Autonomy** (choice, willing endorsement of
  one's own behavior), **Competence** (mastery, effectiveness), **Relatedness** (connection,
  belonging) — are treated as needs, not preferences; supportive social contexts foster them,
  controlling/unsupportive contexts thwart them; thwarting "will have a robust detrimental impact on
  wellness," correlating with reduced well-being, aggression, and relational strain.
- LIMITATION: primary paper PDF unreadable this session; summary page used instead.
- NEW LIFE USE: for each NPC, define what SUPPORTS and what THREATENS each of the three needs, and
  how the character behaviorally reacts when a need is threatened (Section 1C's explicit
  instruction) — never exposed to the player as a score or meter.

**G4. Mischel & Shoda (1995) — Cognitive-Affective Personality System (CAPS)**
- AUTHORS: Walter Mischel, Yuichi Shoda. PUBLICATION: *Psychological Review*, 1995 ("A
  cognitive-affective system theory of personality").
- ACCESS: via `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive-affective_personality_system`, fetched this session
  (secondary source; the primary paper's PDF was located via Semantic Scholar but not fetched).
- SOURCE CLAIM: personality is not a fixed trait producing one behavior — it is a stable **"if
  situation A, then behavior X; if situation B, then behavior Y"** signature. Two people with
  identical trait scores can have completely different behavioral signatures because behavior
  emerges from five interacting cognitive-affective units (encoding, expectancies/beliefs,
  affects, goals/values, competencies/self-regulation) activated differently by different
  situations. Predicting behavior from a trait score alone is explicitly called insufficient.
- LIMITATION: secondary-source synthesis; primary 1995 paper not directly fetched.
- NEW LIFE USE: this is the direct justification for Section 9/10's "current state + event + other
  actor + player move + tendency -> candidate response" formula, and for Section 11's explicit ban
  on linear, trait-only progressions ("trust +1, trust +1, trust +1, REVEAL"). A character's stable
  trait is a *tendency*, never a lookup table for one guaranteed response.

## Cross-cutting note on source confidence

Highest confidence (primary-adjacent, author-controlled, or well-corroborated secondary source):
G3 (theory's own site), G2 (Wikipedia, well-cross-referenced against search-result consensus).
