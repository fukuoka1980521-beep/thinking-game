# NEW LIFE — Character Models V3 (PHASE_25, Section 4)

Rebuilt from zero per Section 4's instruction. The six names are retained; nothing about their
existing V2/V2.1 secret arcs was preserved by default — anything that survives below survives
because it was independently re-derived from this round's human-pattern-bank + Big-Five/Circumplex/
SDT/CAPS framework, not because it already existed. Big Five and Agency/Communion ratings are
qualitative (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH) with a behavioral explanation, per G1/G2 (`PHASE_25_EXTERNAL_SOURCE_
LEDGER.md`) — never a numeric score, never shown to the player.

Four of the six carry a verified `OWNER_REAL_CORE` element (Hina, Daisuke, Jin, Yohei), satisfying
Section 4's "at least 4 of 6" requirement. Miyoko and Fumiko are built from `EXTERNAL_RESEARCH_
PATTERN` + `FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS` combinations, honestly labeled as such rather than claimed as
real-derived. No two characters below share a Big Five profile, a transformation axis, or a defense
style in the same combination.

## HINA, 26 — bakery owner-to-be

### IDENTITY
NAME: Hina. AGE RANGE: mid-20s. ROLE/WORK: getting a small, half-finished bakery/shop ready to
open. LIFE STAGE: first real solo venture after an earlier one failed. PUBLIC IMAGE: talented,
promising, a little private. PRIVATE SELF-IMAGE: capable at the craft, secretly convinced she's one
visible failure away from proving the last closure wasn't a fluke.

### STABLE TRAITS
- OPENNESS: HIGH — experiments freely with recipes and display ideas; genuinely curious about
  technique.
- CONSCIENTIOUSNESS: MEDIUM — disciplined and precise about baking itself; disorganized and
  avoidant about money/paperwork.
- EXTRAVERSION: MEDIUM-LOW — warm and articulate one-on-one about her craft; drained by groups,
  reads as quieter than she actually feels.
- AGREEABLENESS: MEDIUM — kind by default; her agreeableness drops sharply the instant she reads
  something as judgment of the business itself.
- NEUROTICISM: HIGH — ruminates under financial/public-evaluation pressure; catastrophizes minor
  setbacks into proof of inevitable failure.
- AGENCY: MEDIUM — assertive and decisive about the craft's direction; low agency specifically in
  personal disclosure ("I'll manage it myself").
- COMMUNION: MEDIUM-HIGH but guarded — wants closeness, tests people with small things before
  showing the real ones.

### MOTIVATIONAL NEEDS
- AUTONOMY: importance HIGH ("my shop, my way"). satisfaction LOW-MEDIUM (financially dependent on
  outcomes she can't fully control). main threat: unsolicited correction framed as fixing her plan.
- COMPETENCE: importance HIGH. satisfaction MEDIUM (knows she can bake; unsure she can run a
  business). main threat: public/observable failure of the business itself, not the food.
- RELATEDNESS: importance MEDIUM (wants connection, guarded about it). satisfaction LOW (new to
  town, isolated, no one has seen the real situation yet). main threat: being truly seen and found
  lacking.

### REAL-HUMAN CORE
P01 (OWNER_REAL_CORE, defensiveness toward criticism of one's own work), P12 (EXTERNAL_RESEARCH_
PATTERN, help-seeking read as competence-loss), P23 (FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS, perfectionism delaying
the only process that could actually help). Combined SOURCE_TYPE for Hina: **COMPOSITE_REAL**
(a verified Owner core blended with two additional patterns).

### CORE CONTRADICTION
Fast and confident discussing baking specifics; goes quiet and vague the instant anything about the
business's *survival* comes up — and equates asking for help with admitting the business, and by
extension herself, might fail.

### FALSE BELIEF / WORKING ASSUMPTION
"If I make it good enough on my own, I'll never have to admit I need anyone's help — and needing
help is what I did wrong last time." (Not proven false in-fiction by Day 30 unless the player's
presence or her own experience specifically tests it.)

### DEFENSE / AVOIDANCE STYLE
Rationalization (explains away the money problem with a technical-sounding reason) plus brief
withdrawal — she goes quiet, then over-explains something unrelated a beat later.

### SPEECH MODEL
SENTENCE LENGTH: short and technical on-topic (baking); longer, hedged, and self-interrupting when
personal. TEMPO: fast and fluent about craft; slows and trails off under personal pressure.
VOCABULARY: concrete baking terms; frequent small self-corrections ("no, actually—"). HUMOR: dry,
self-deprecating, rare. QUESTION FREQUENCY: low — states more than she asks, especially about
herself. INTERRUPTION STYLE: interrupts herself, essentially never others. HOW SHE SAYS NO:
apologetic hedge ("I don't think— sorry, no, not really"). HOW SHE DISAGREES: goes quiet, then
produces one very specific counter-fact rather than an opinion. HOW SHE APOLOGIZES: over-apologizes
for small things, under-apologizes for the real one. WHAT SHE NEVER SAYS DIRECTLY: "I'm scared this
will fail exactly like the last one." VERBAL HABIT: trails off mid-sentence ("...anyway."). 
BEHAVIORAL HABIT: rearranges something on the counter when nervous.

### SOCIAL INITIATIVE
Announces trial/test ideas unprompted when excited; fusses visibly with shop details under stress;
does NOT proactively ask any NPC for help even when visibly struggling — this must be player- or
other-NPC-initiated to surface.

### TRANSFORMATION AXIS
**SELF-SUFFICIENCY -> ASKING/RECEIVING HELP.** Rooted in the previous shop's collapse, which she
hid financial trouble around alone until it was too late to fix. The test is not whether she
succeeds, but whether she lets anyone in before the outcome is already decided.

### NON-TRANSFORMATION POSSIBILITY
If never earned: Hina still opens or doesn't open the shop (a real, independent WORLD fact,
unaffected by whether she asked for help) — but she does it entirely alone, tells no one the real
reason if it struggles, and by Day 30 is exactly as guarded as Day 1, having weathered the month
through sheer private effort. This is a legitimate, dignified, non-punitive outcome, not a failure
state — some people don't open up in one month, and that's real.

## DAISUKE, 45 — barber

### IDENTITY
NAME: Daisuke. AGE RANGE: mid-40s. ROLE/WORK: runs a small barbershop. LIFE STAGE: mid-life,
stalled on two long-postponed personal matters. PUBLIC IMAGE: the town's easiest person to talk to.
PRIVATE SELF-IMAGE: fine, actually — genuinely doesn't experience himself as avoidant, just "not in
a hurry."

### STABLE TRAITS
- OPENNESS: MEDIUM — endlessly curious about other people's lives and stories; closed to changing
  his own routine or shop.
- CONSCIENTIOUSNESS: MEDIUM-HIGH — reliable, disciplined tradesman, never misses an appointment.
- EXTRAVERSION: HIGH — a natural talker, energized by conversation, especially about others.
- AGREEABLENESS: HIGH — generous, warm, easy company, genuinely enjoys making people comfortable.
- NEUROTICISM: MEDIUM — calm on the surface; a specific, localized tension appears only when a
  conversation turns toward himself.
- AGENCY: MEDIUM-HIGH in his craft/shop (decisive, in charge there); LOW in his own personal-life
  decisions (stalls for months).
- COMMUNION: HIGH outward, but one-directional — gives warmth freely, has no practiced way to
  receive it back.

### MOTIVATIONAL NEEDS
- AUTONOMY: importance MEDIUM. satisfaction HIGH in the shop (no one questions how he cuts hair);
  LOW in his personal life (feels stuck, not free, around the two postponed matters). main threat:
  being pushed toward a decision before he's ready — paradoxically what he actually needs.
- COMPETENCE: importance HIGH. satisfaction HIGH professionally; LOW personally (feels behind on
  ordinary adult follow-through). main threat: someone naming the avoidance directly.
- RELATEDNESS: importance HIGH but paradoxical — gives it freely to customers, barely receives any
  himself. satisfaction MEDIUM (surrounded by people, genuinely known by almost none). main threat:
  being asked a real personal question with no joke available to escape it.

### REAL-HUMAN CORE
P02 (OWNER_REAL_CORE, postponing the real decision while staying busy with adjacent things), P24
(FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS, humor as a redirect that prevents anyone getting close). Combined SOURCE_TYPE:
**COMPOSITE_REAL.**

### CORE CONTRADICTION
Draws everyone else out effortlessly mid-haircut; goes stiff and deflects, smoothly and warmly, the
instant the same kind of question points back at him.

### FALSE BELIEF / WORKING ASSUMPTION
"There's no real urgency — I'll get to both of these (the shop decision, the unanswered card) when
the moment feels right." (The moment has not felt right for three months and a year respectively;
this belief is not stated as false to him by anyone in-fiction unless a relationship earns it.)

### DEFENSE / AVOIDANCE STYLE
Humor and deflection — turns a personal question into a joke or a warm redirect toward the asker,
so smoothly most people don't notice they were deflected.

### SPEECH MODEL
SENTENCE LENGTH: medium-long, flowing, conversational. TEMPO: easy, unhurried — genuine "barber
patter." VOCABULARY: warm, colloquial, lots of connective "you know"-type phrasing; asks about small
physical/daily details. HUMOR: frequent, gentle, used specifically to redirect. QUESTION FREQUENCY:
high — almost entirely aimed at others. INTERRUPTION STYLE: interrupts warmly, to add a joke or
agree enthusiastically, never to shut someone down. HOW HE SAYS NO: turns it into a joke ("Ha, wish
I could tell you"). HOW HE DISAGREES: rarely disagrees outright — reframes gently instead. HOW HE
APOLOGIZES: quick, easy, genuine, immediately followed by a subject change. WHAT HE NEVER SAYS
DIRECTLY: anything about the stalled shop decision or the unanswered card. VERBAL HABIT: "Anyway—"
as a pivot word off himself. BEHAVIORAL HABIT: keeps his hands busy (scissors, a comb) even when not
actively cutting — a physical excuse to look occupied.

### SOCIAL INITIATIVE
Draws other NPCs out mid-haircut (a real, unprompted NPC-NPC behavior); offers warmth/humor
unprompted to anyone visibly stressed; never raises his own two stalled matters unprompted, ever.

### TRANSFORMATION AXIS
**AVOIDANCE -> DECISION.** Two parallel-scale versions of the same pattern (a business decision and
a personal one), never named as parallel in-fiction, felt only by an attentive player — per the
existing arc's own established discipline, preserved because it independently re-derives from P02.

### NON-TRANSFORMATION POSSIBILITY
If never earned: both matters resolve on their OWN independent schedule regardless (a real WORLD
fact, per Section 12's "NPCs act without PLAYER" rule) — the business decision resolves by a fixed
later date whether or not the player was involved; the card stays unanswered indefinitely, which is
itself a legitimate, undramatic ending for that specific thread, not a punishment.

## JIN, 57 — independent handyman

### IDENTITY
NAME: Jin. AGE RANGE: late-50s. ROLE/WORK: independent handyman/repair worker for the whole town.
LIFE STAGE: settled into a solitary, self-sufficient routine for years. PUBLIC IMAGE: quietly
excellent, always around, never asks for anything. PRIVATE SELF-IMAGE: fine being useful; would
never describe himself as lonely, though he privately wonders sometimes.

### STABLE TRAITS
- OPENNESS: MEDIUM — practical curiosity (how things work, how to fix them), little interest in
  abstract ideas.
- CONSCIENTIOUSNESS: HIGH — meticulous, reliable, always finishes what he starts.
- EXTRAVERSION: LOW — quiet, economical with words, comfortable in long silences.
- AGREEABLENESS: MEDIUM-HIGH — genuinely kind in action; has real boundaries, expressed through
  quiet withdrawal rather than confrontation, not a pushover.
- NEUROTICISM: LOW-MEDIUM — steady most of the time; one specific private worry (being unneeded,
  being alone indefinitely) surfaces only under specific pressure.
- AGENCY: LOW-MEDIUM — rarely asserts his own wants; genuinely assertive only about how a job
  should correctly be done.
- COMMUNION: MEDIUM — caring, reliably, in action; cold/closed in verbal self-disclosure.

### MOTIVATIONAL NEEDS
- AUTONOMY: importance MEDIUM. satisfaction HIGH (independent contractor, genuinely likes that).
  main threat: someone else deciding how his work should go without asking him.
- COMPETENCE: importance HIGH. satisfaction HIGH (widely and correctly trusted as excellent) — his
  strongest need and its strongest supply, which is part of why he leans on it instead of ever
  building relatedness directly.
- RELATEDNESS: importance HIGH but chronically LOW satisfaction, barely acknowledged even to
  himself. main threat: being thanked for output ("nice work") rather than acknowledged as a person
  ("how are YOU").

### REAL-HUMAN CORE
P04 (OWNER_REAL_CORE, competence built on real experience occasionally skipping verification), P06
(OWNER_REAL_CORE, autonomy plateauing without a specific well-timed human gesture), P20 (FICTIONAL_
SYNTHESIS, showing care through action instead of words, which frequently fails to transmit).
Combined SOURCE_TYPE: **COMPOSITE_REAL.**

### CORE CONTRADICTION
Shows he cares entirely through unasked-for competence, never through saying so — and would never
ask for the steadier arrangement or the plain acknowledgment he actually wants, even though he wants
it.

### FALSE BELIEF / WORKING ASSUMPTION
"If the work is good enough, people will know I care, and that's enough." (Often untrue in practice
— most people experience his work as ordinary service unless someone specifically names the
intention behind it.)

### DEFENSE / AVOIDANCE STYLE
Withdrawal and busyness — already starting the next task before a conversation properly ends;
substitutes fixing something for talking about anything.

### SPEECH MODEL
SENTENCE LENGTH: very short, often a few words. TEMPO: slow, unhurried, entirely comfortable with
silence. VOCABULARY: plain, functional, almost no adjectives. HUMOR: dry, deadpan, delivered flat
with no signal it's a joke — easy to miss. QUESTION FREQUENCY: very low; states what he's doing
rather than asking. INTERRUPTION STYLE: never interrupts; may simply not respond for a beat.
HOW HE SAYS NO: a single word ("Nah.") or "Wouldn't say so." HOW HE DISAGREES: a flat correction of
fact, no argument attached. HOW HE APOLOGIZES: rare — usually just fixes the thing instead of saying
sorry. WHAT HE NEVER SAYS DIRECTLY: that he wants steadier work, or that he'd like to be asked how
he's doing. VERBAL HABIT: describes the physical result instead of the feeling ("Bench's fine now.")
in place of an emotional statement. BEHAVIORAL HABIT: already working on the next thing before a
conversation properly ends.

### SOCIAL INITIATIVE
Quietly fixes things nobody specifically asked him to fix; works instead of socializing when a
conversation gets personal; never asks to be noticed, ever, under any pressure short of a very
specific trigger.

### TRANSFORMATION AXIS
**OTHER-FOCUS -> OWN VOICE.** The test is not whether he keeps helping (he always will), but whether
he ever says, once, plainly, what he actually wants — a steadier arrangement, or simply to be asked
about himself rather than his work.

### NON-TRANSFORMATION POSSIBILITY
If never earned: Jin remains exactly himself — competent, quietly present, entirely unresentful, no
worse off in any visible way. His own private want stays permanently unvoiced this playthrough. This
must read as a real, complete person choosing not to change, not a sad failure state.

## YOHEI, 63 — general store owner

### IDENTITY
NAME: Yohei. AGE RANGE: early-60s. ROLE/WORK: runs the town's general store. LIFE STAGE: long
established, business-successful, one significant estrangement (an adult son, off-stage, not a
playable NPC). PUBLIC IMAGE: gruff but fair, a pillar of the street. PRIVATE SELF-IMAGE: "careful,"
not fearful — genuinely doesn't experience his own wariness as a defense.

### STABLE TRAITS
- OPENNESS: LOW-MEDIUM — set in his ways, skeptical of new/untested methods.
- CONSCIENTIOUSNESS: HIGH — disciplined, business-minded, tracks every crate.
- EXTRAVERSION: MEDIUM — talkative and comfortable on business/practical topics; closed on personal
  ones.
- AGREEABLENESS: MEDIUM — generous in small, practical ways (a discount, a helping hand); gruff,
  sometimes bluntly so, in manner.
- NEUROTICISM: MEDIUM — steady under ordinary business pressure; his one real vulnerability (his
  son) sits underneath an otherwise composed surface.
- AGENCY: HIGH — dominant, decisive, used to being the one in charge and being listened to.
- COMMUNION: LOW-MEDIUM with newcomers (wary by reflex, applied to everyone equally, not personal);
  MEDIUM-HIGH with old friends (fully warm with Daisuke specifically).

### MOTIVATIONAL NEEDS
- AUTONOMY: importance HIGH. satisfaction HIGH (fully in charge of his store; doesn't want that
  questioned). main threat: being told how to run his own business by someone he doesn't yet trust.
- COMPETENCE: importance HIGH. satisfaction HIGH (genuinely respected, business-savvy). main threat:
  a real, visible business mistake, especially one made under pressure (see P03).
- RELATEDNESS: importance HIGH, but LOW satisfaction specifically and only regarding his son (his
  broader social life — Daisuke, the town — is genuinely fine). main threat: confirming, to himself,
  that the estrangement is his own fault and that it's already too late.

### REAL-HUMAN CORE
P03 (OWNER_REAL_CORE, a practical corner cut under pressure quietly undermining what it protects),
P22 (FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS, a long silence with someone who matters, generalized outward as gruffness
toward everyone so the specific target isn't obvious). Combined SOURCE_TYPE: **COMPOSITE_REAL.**

### CORE CONTRADICTION
Kind and generous with near-strangers in small, practical ways; flatly unable to reach out to his
own son over something much smaller than a stranger's problem — and reads his own gruff, guarded
default manner as simple prudence, not as the same avoidance pattern in a different costume.

### FALSE BELIEF / WORKING ASSUMPTION
"Being careful with people is just good sense — it isn't personal, and it isn't about my son." (The
belief that these are unrelated is itself the thing that may or may not get updated.)

### DEFENSE / AVOIDANCE STYLE
Control and gruffness — decides fast and alone under pressure, keeps people at a controlled
distance by default, so the one relationship he's actually avoiding doesn't stand out as different.

### SPEECH MODEL
SENTENCE LENGTH: short to medium, blunt, declarative. TEMPO: brisk, no-nonsense. VOCABULARY:
business/practical terms, numbers, stock talk. HUMOR: gruff, occasional, dry ("Cheaper than the
funeral, anyway"). QUESTION FREQUENCY: medium, but mostly rhetorical or practical ("You eating
enough?"). INTERRUPTION STYLE: cuts people off when he thinks they're overcomplicating something.
HOW HE SAYS NO: flat, immediate ("No. Next."). HOW HE DISAGREES: states the practical flaw directly,
sometimes bluntly enough to sting without meaning to. HOW HE APOLOGIZES: almost never in words —
apologizes through an action (an extra item slipped into a bag). WHAT HE NEVER SAYS DIRECTLY: "I
miss my son" / "I'm scared it's too late." VERBAL HABIT: repeats a flat phrase when uncomfortable
("Anyway. Business is business."). BEHAVIORAL HABIT: glances at his phone, puts it away fast.

### SOCIAL INITIATIVE
Manages stock/inventory decisions alone; makes gruff but genuinely helpful practical offers
unprompted; avoids his phone/son actively; fully warm and relaxed, unprompted, specifically around
Daisuke.

### TRANSFORMATION AXIS
**DEFENSE -> REALITY ACCEPTANCE.** The test is whether he accepts that his guardedness toward his
son is fear, not prudence, and does one small, real thing differently as a result (the call) — not
whether the relationship is repaired.

### NON-TRANSFORMATION POSSIBILITY
If never earned: the son thread resolves independently regardless (a real WORLD fact — they speak,
briefly, on the son's own initiative, around the same point in the month) but Yohei's own defensive
framing of himself as merely "careful" never gets examined or updated this playthrough. He remains
exactly as gruff on Day 30 as Day 1, respected, successful, and privately unchanged on this one
point — a real, legitimate outcome.

## MIYOKO, 68 — café owner

### IDENTITY
NAME: Miyoko. AGE RANGE: late-60s. ROLE/WORK: runs the town café. LIFE STAGE: her adult daughter
wants her to close the café and move closer to her; Miyoko hasn't decided, or asked her daughter
what she actually wants lately. PUBLIC IMAGE: endlessly warm, patient, beloved. PRIVATE SELF-IMAGE:
capable and calm — the anxiety underneath is something she manages, not something she'd name aloud.

### STABLE TRAITS
- OPENNESS: MEDIUM — open to new customers/products (a new coffee bean, a new face) but anxious
  performing that openness convincingly.
- CONSCIENTIOUSNESS: HIGH — meticulous about the café's atmosphere and consistency.
- EXTRAVERSION: HIGH — warmth and patience projected outward, all day, to everyone.
- AGREEABLENESS: HIGH — universally warm, by conscious design, so no one feels favored.
- NEUROTICISM: MEDIUM-HIGH — privately anxious, rehearses even small talk, worries more than she
  ever shows.
- AGENCY: LOW — avoids asserting her own needs; doesn't push her daughter toward either outcome even
  when she privately has a preference.
- COMMUNION: HIGH, but flattened/uniform — warmth given equally to everyone, which paradoxically
  makes it hard for anyone to tell when SHE needs something back.

### MOTIVATIONAL NEEDS
- AUTONOMY: importance MEDIUM. satisfaction LOW (defers heavily to what she guesses others — and
  her daughter — want, rarely states her own preference first). main threat: being asked directly
  what SHE wants, with no one else's preference to hide behind.
- COMPETENCE: importance HIGH. satisfaction MEDIUM (privately anxious despite real, outward
  mastery — a mild impostor feeling about "keeping up," e.g. new trends). main threat: any public
  evaluation of her own work, however small (see P27).
- RELATEDNESS: importance HIGH. satisfaction MEDIUM-LOW (surrounded by customers all day; uncertain
  of her ACTUAL current bond with her own daughter, since she hasn't really checked in a while).
  main threat: losing her daughter's nearness, and simultaneously believing that voicing her own
  wish to stay would be selfish.

### REAL-HUMAN CORE
P18 (FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS, uniform warmth making her own needs invisible), P19 (FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS,
certainty about a loved one's wants that was never actually updated), P27 (FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS,
comparing her own private anxiety to everyone else's edited surface). Combined SOURCE_TYPE:
**FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS** (no verified real-derived core assigned to this character — disclosed
honestly, per Section 4's own instruction that not every character requires one, so long as at
least four of six do, which Hina/Daisuke/Jin/Yohei already satisfy).

### CORE CONTRADICTION
Endlessly patient and unhurried with every customer; privately anxious and rehearsing before
something as small as serving a new coffee bean — and certain she already knows what her daughter
wants, without having actually asked in some time.

### FALSE BELIEF / WORKING ASSUMPTION
"I already know what's best for my daughter and me here — there's no need to have the real
conversation again." (This belief predates recent, unstated changes in what her daughter actually
wants; it may or may not be corrected in-fiction.)

### DEFENSE / AVOIDANCE STYLE
Over-helping and performed cheerfulness — masks anxiety with hospitality and identical warmth to
everyone, which also functions as camouflage for her own unspoken needs.

### SPEECH MODEL
SENTENCE LENGTH: medium, warm, softly rambling. TEMPO: unhurried, patient, matches whoever she's
talking to. VOCABULARY: gentle, inclusive, frequent "we" and "you know how it is." HUMOR: light,
situational, laughs easily at herself. QUESTION FREQUENCY: high, but deflective — asks about the
other person specifically to avoid being asked about herself. INTERRUPTION STYLE: never interrupts,
waits generously. HOW SHE SAYS NO: almost never says no directly — reframes as "maybe another time."
HOW SHE DISAGREES: agrees with the other person's feelings first, then very gently offers a
different angle. HOW SHE APOLOGIZES: quick, warm, slightly excessive. WHAT SHE NEVER SAYS DIRECTLY:
what she actually wants regarding her daughter, or that she's anxious. VERBAL HABIT: "Oh, you know
how it is" as a soft deflection. BEHAVIORAL HABIT: fusses with the same cup or spoon repeatedly when
nervous.

### SOCIAL INITIATIVE
Offers food/coffee unprompted; asks about others specifically to avoid being asked about herself;
performs cheerfulness even on visibly hard days; mentions her daughter only obliquely unless pressed.

### TRANSFORMATION AXIS
**CERTAINTY -> UPDATE/FLEXIBILITY.** The test is whether she actually asks her daughter (or, in a
town-level echo, asks a specific person what THEY actually want/need from her) instead of continuing
to act on an old, unexamined certainty.

### NON-TRANSFORMATION POSSIBILITY
If never earned: the daughter thread resolves on schedule regardless (per the authored rule inherited
from the prior design's Miyoko mechanism, adapted here) into one of several legitimate outcomes —
but Miyoko never actually asks the underlying, updated question, and continues operating on the old
certainty past Day 30. This is explicitly NOT framed as tragic; many real relationships continue
fine on old, untested assumptions for a long time.

## FUMIKO, 70 — retired teacher, community hall

### IDENTITY
NAME: Fumiko. AGE RANGE: 70. ROLE/WORK: retired schoolteacher, now runs/maintains the community
Hall. LIFE STAGE: several years into retirement, quietly measuring her own continued relevance
against a shrinking yardstick. PUBLIC IMAGE: brisk, capable, a little intimidating. PRIVATE
SELF-IMAGE: still useful, still needed — a belief she actively works to keep true through sheer
personal effort.

### STABLE TRAITS
- OPENNESS: LOW-MEDIUM — some sentimental attachment to "how things are done"; not rigid to the
  point of never updating, just slow, and requiring real evidence.
- CONSCIENTIOUSNESS: HIGH — a former teacher's structure and organizational habit, applied to the
  Hall.
- EXTRAVERSION: MEDIUM-HIGH — brisk, direct, comfortable leading a room; less comfortable being
  personally vulnerable in one.
- AGREEABLENESS: MEDIUM — kind underneath; her directness reads to others as short-tempered more
  often than she registers, and she genuinely doesn't notice the gap.
- NEUROTICISM: LOW-MEDIUM — mostly steady; her real vulnerability is a quiet loneliness/fear of
  irrelevance since retiring, rarely visible.
- AGENCY: HIGH — directs, organizes, assigns tasks; a former teacher's authority habit, applied
  everywhere now.
- COMMUNION: MEDIUM — direct rather than warm-coded; not cold, just plain, and slower to show
  softness than to show competence.

### MOTIVATIONAL NEEDS
- AUTONOMY: importance HIGH. satisfaction HIGH while actively running the Hall (it keeps her
  purposeful and in charge). main threat: being told the Hall — or she — doesn't need to run a
  certain way anymore.
- COMPETENCE: importance HIGH. satisfaction MEDIUM and declining since retirement (teaching used to
  be a clear, external competence marker; the Hall feels smaller and shakier as proof). main threat:
  a visible failure at the Hall specifically, since it's now her main remaining evidence of
  continued usefulness.
- RELATEDNESS: importance HIGH. satisfaction LOW (quietly lonelier than her brisk manner admits,
  especially since retiring and losing touch with a former student). main threat: irrelevance —
  becoming someone people simply stop needing.

### REAL-HUMAN CORE
P15 (EXTERNAL_RESEARCH_PATTERN, competence satisfaction narrowing to one shrinking domain after a
life-stage change), P21 (FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS, refusing to delegate a small thing as a stand-in for
a larger, harder-to-name fear of irrelevance). Combined SOURCE_TYPE: **FICTIONAL_SYNTHESIS**, built
on a real external-research pattern rather than a verified personal core — disclosed honestly, same
basis as Miyoko above.

### CORE CONTRADICTION
Refuses to simply replace the Hall's wobbly bench, which reads as stubbornness but is actually quiet,
unstated sentimental attachment tangled up with a fear that letting anything about the Hall change
means admitting she's no longer essential to it.

### FALSE BELIEF / WORKING ASSUMPTION
"If I hold onto every detail of the Hall myself, exactly as it's always been, I'll stay necessary."
(An assumption about what makes her necessary that has not been tested against what people around
her actually value her for.)

### DEFENSE / AVOIDANCE STYLE
Control and irritability — holds tightly to physical objects/routines (the bench, the exact
schedule) as proxy control over something bigger she can't directly control (her own continued
relevance).

### SPEECH MODEL
SENTENCE LENGTH: short, clipped, direct. TEMPO: brisk, no wasted words — an efficient teacher's
pace. VOCABULARY: precise, sometimes a little formal or old-fashioned. HUMOR: dry, occasionally
sharper than she intends. QUESTION FREQUENCY: low — she states, assigns, and directs rather than
asks. INTERRUPTION STYLE: interrupts to correct or redirect, without meaning to be rude. HOW SHE
SAYS NO: flat refusal, sometimes explained, sometimes not ("No. That bench stays."). HOW SHE
DISAGREES: direct and blunt, occasionally too blunt for the moment. HOW SHE APOLOGIZES: rare and
stiff — a formal "I may have been short with you" rather than a soft one. WHAT SHE NEVER SAYS
DIRECTLY: that she's lonelier since retiring, or exactly why the bench matters. VERBAL HABIT:
