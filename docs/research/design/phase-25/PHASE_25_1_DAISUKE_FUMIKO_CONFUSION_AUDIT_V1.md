## Root cause, stated plainly

**Every single Daisuke error is a SHORT, FACTUAL, or DRY-HUMOR line where his warmth-toward-the-
other-person trait had no occasion to appear.** His model's distinguishing feature (constant,
outward-directed curiosity and warmth) only shows up in his longer lines; his short lines default to
a generic dry/clipped register that happens to overlap with Fumiko's baseline. **The one Fumiko
error is the mirror case**: a short deflection with none of her distinguishing feature (procedural
precision, responsibility/sequence framing) present, defaulting to a generic dry-deflection register
that happens to overlap with Daisuke's defense style. **Neither character was actually wrong to
write — the failure is that their distinguishing trait was written only into their LONG lines,
leaving their SHORT lines personality-neutral.** This confirms the diagnosis already reached in
`NEWLIFE_PHASE_25_VOICE_BLIND_TEST_RESULTS_V1.md` and sharpens it: the fix is not "make Daisuke
funnier" or "make Fumiko stricter" (surface catchphrase fixes, explicitly forbidden by this task) —
it is **carrying each one's own way of noticing the world into every line length, including one-word
and one-sentence answers.**

## Section 3 — the distinction, before wording

| | DAISUKE | FUMIKO |
|---|---|---|
| **Notices first** | The physical, concrete condition of things and people — wear on a tool, how someone's sitting, whether someone seems off in their day-to-day rhythm. Reads people through small physical tells, not their words. | Whether something was actually agreed to, by whom, and whether responsibility/sequence is clear. Reads situations as "who is accountable for what, in what order." |
| **Wants to avoid** | Being the one who has to say something serious out loud, especially about himself; making a moment heavier than it needs to be. | Ambiguity going unaddressed; a small thing being nobody's job and becoming everyone's problem later. |
| **Social risk feared** | Being caught with "nothing to say" if a real question lands and no joke is ready — read as evasive or unreliable. | Being seen as no longer capable of managing things — a step toward irrelevance, needing to hand things off. |
| **What makes them speak first** | Noticing someone's physical/practical state (tired, off, a tool that's wrong) — comments on THAT, then asks a personal question about the other person, never himself. | Noticing an unclear commitment, an approaching date, or an unowned task — states it plainly, often assigning it to someone on the spot. |
| **When they don't know the answer** | Deflects with humor, then pivots to ask the other person something instead — buys time through warmth outward. | States plainly that she doesn't know YET, and immediately proposes how/when it'll be found out — turns not-knowing into a next procedural step, never hides it. |

**Why this repairs the short-answer collapse**: even a one-word or one-sentence Daisuke line can now
carry a *noticed physical detail* or a *redirect toward the other person* — the trait doesn't require
length, it requires being anchored to what he specifically notices. Even a one-sentence Fumiko line
can carry a *responsibility/sequence frame* or a *plain "not yet, here's how we'll find out"* — same
principle. Neither requires a catchphrase; both require the underlying attention pattern to survive
compression.

Big Five profile, P02/P24 real-human core, defense (humor/deflection), SPEECH MODEL fields.

**Sharpened, behaviorally, not cosmetically**: every line — long or short — should be checkable
against at least one of: (a) a concrete object/repair/physical-condition observation ("that hinge is
looser than it was last week," "you're favoring that shoulder again"); (b) practical improvisation
(fixing/adjusting something mid-conversation, not just talking); (c) humor specifically as
deflection FROM a personal question TOWARD the other person, not humor in a vacuum; (d) visible

specificity the moment a REAL deadline lands (a rare, load-bearing exception to his usual vagueness —
reserved for genuinely high-stakes moments, not overused); (f) talking about work/tools as a
substitute for talking about feelings. **Never**: generic cheerful-man filler, generic "wise
craftsman" aphorisms, joking for its own sake without a redirect-away-from-self function.

## Section 5 — Fumiko's distinctive core (preserved role and psychology; behavior sharpened)

Preserved unchanged from `NEWLIFE_CHARACTER_MODELS_V3.md`: 70, retired teacher/Hall, CONTROL->
DELEGATION axis, Big Five profile, P15/P21 core, defense (control/irritability), SPEECH MODEL
fields.

**Sharpened, behaviorally**: every line should be checkable against at least one of: (a)
responsibility/ownership framing ("who agreed to that," "that was never actually assigned to
anyone"); (b) sequence/dates ("that was supposed to happen before the delivery, not after"); (c)
naming ambiguity in a commitment out loud, plainly, without softening it; (d) institutional/communal
consequence framing (what happens to the Hall/the town, not just to her personally); (e) correcting
imprecise wording, briefly, almost reflexively; (f) a personal preference that hides behind a
procedural justification ("the schedule doesn't allow it" standing in for "I'd rather not"); (g)
difficulty actually handing a task to someone else, shown through OVER-specifying instructions
rather than simply refusing to delegate. **Never**: generic strict-older-woman scolding, generic
schoolteacher voice, rule-enforcement for its own sake with no responsibility/sequence logic behind
it. **Her warmth, when it appears, looks different from Hina's (guarded/technical) and Miyoko's
(uniform/deflective)**: Fumiko's warmth shows up as unusually precise, personal attention to a
detail only someone who was really paying attention would have noticed — not through soft words.
