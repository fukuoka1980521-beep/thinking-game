## Strongest current interpretation

The data now support a narrower and more informative model:

1. Methodological framing robustly changes the **linguistic / organizational form of free-form research planning**.
2. That signal replicates across tasks and models.
3. The signal does **not automatically transfer** into coarse structured interpretation, constrained evidence selection, or structured attention allocation.
4. Therefore the Study A effect cannot yet be treated as evidence that methodological framing changes downstream scientific decisions or evidence-acquisition policy.

This is a boundary finding, not a failure of the program.

## Mechanism hypotheses still open

At least three explanations remain compatible with the data:

### H1 — representational-expression account
Method framing mainly changes how the model articulates and organizes a research plan.

### H2 — latent-policy-with-lossy-readout account
Method framing changes planning policy, but the B/C/D structured instruments discard the dimensions where that policy differs.

### H3 — plan-mediated transfer account
Direct framing may not strongly change constrained choices, but a generated free-form plan may carry method-specific policy into later actions.

The existing data distinguish H1/H2/H3 only partially.

## Next decisive study: Study E — Plan-to-Action Transfer

Target:

M -> Z_plan -> A_action

Procedure concept:
1. Planner receives the methodological frame and generates a free-form plan.
2. Direct method labels are deterministically removed from the plan before execution.
3. A fresh neutral executor receives the sanitized plan but no methodological frame.
4. The executor acts in a fixed evidence-choice environment.
5. Test whether downstream action paths retain recoverable information about the original planner frame across worlds.

This directly tests whether the replicated plan signature has behavioral consequences.

## Claim ceiling

Current allowed conclusion:

> Methodological framing produces a replicated, task-general, model-replicable signature in free-form research planning, but current structured calibrations do not show that the signature directly survives into fixed-evidence interpretation or constrained evidence-selection behavior.

Not allowed:
- methodological framing universally changes reasoning;
- hidden chain-of-thought was observed;
- one methodology is superior;
- the Study A signature necessarily improves research quality;
- B/C/D calibration failures prove downstream invariance.
