# Methodological Framing and Research Trajectories 窶・Theoretical Model

## Core question

When the research objective is held fixed, does changing only the methodological framing alter the observable research trajectory of a large language model?

Let the fixed research objective be (G), the methodological instruction be (M), the resulting research plan state be (Z_{plan}), the evidence selected or generated be (E), and the final conclusion state be (Y).

The proposed causal structure is:

[
G + M
竊・Z_{plan}
竊・E
竊・Y
]

with possible direct effects:

[
M
竊・E; M
竊・Y.
]

The scientific target is **not** an unobservable internal cognitive variable. It is a reproducible black-box effect of randomized methodological instructions on externally measurable research behavior.

## Main claim ceiling

Allowed:
- methodological framing changes observable problem decomposition;
- methodological framing changes variables, hypotheses, controls, evidence choices, stopping rules, and conclusions;
- some of those effects may be mediated through altered evidence acquisition.

Not allowed without internal access:
- a specific hidden neural variable was activated;
- the model literally reasoned as a human Bayesian/scientist/programmer;
- an internal search trajectory is directly observed.

## Research-state representation

Define a structured research-plan state

[
Z_{plan}=[
D,V,H,C,F,Q,T,S,U
]
]

where:

- (D): problem decomposition structure;
- (V): variables and state representation selected;
- (H): hypotheses and alternatives;
- (C): controls and counterfactuals;
- (F): falsification criteria;
- (Q): evidence/search queries and acquisition strategy;
- (T): proposed tests or experiments;
- (S): stopping/decision rules;
- (U): uncertainty and claim-strength policy.

A later execution-state vector can add:

[
Z_{exec}=[E_{selected},A_{executed},O_{observed},Y_{conclusion}].
]

## Three-study decomposition

### Study A 窶・Plan-only framing effect

Hold fixed:
- objective;
- model;
- endpoint settings;
- context;
- tool access = none;
- evidence = none;
- response format and budget.

Randomize only (M).

Estimate:

[
M
竊・Z_{plan}.
]

This is the cleanest causal test of whether methodological framing changes research style before evidence enters.

### Study B 窶・Fixed-evidence interpretation effect

Give every condition the identical frozen evidence packet.

Estimate:

[
M
竊・Y | E=E^*.
]

This isolates interpretation and synthesis from evidence acquisition.

### Study C 窶・Active research trajectory

Give every condition the same tool set, source universe, time/call/token budget, and stopping budget, while allowing evidence selection.

Estimate the full path:

[
M
竊・Z_{plan}
竊・E_{selected}
竊・Y.
]

This tests whether framing changes what becomes observable and whether those evidence-selection differences mediate conclusion differences.

## Why this is not merely prompt engineering

Standard prompt-engineering evaluation commonly asks which prompt yields better accuracy or task performance.

This project asks a different question:

> Under the same research objective, which parts of the scientific workflow change when the methodological frame changes?

The dependent variables are therefore not only correctness or final answer, but the structure of research behavior itself.

## Policy-level formulation for active research

For Study C, the strongest black-box formulation is not an unobserved 窶彡ognitive trajectory,窶・but a method-conditioned observable research policy.

Let:
- H_t = the externally visible research history available at step t;
- M = methodological frame;
- A_t = the next observable research action, such as selecting evidence, requesting an analysis, running an allowed experiment, or stopping.

The measurable object is:

P(A_t | H_t, M)

If, under matched histories and the same available action space,

P(A_t | H_t, M1) != P(A_t | H_t, M2),

then methodological framing has changed the observable action policy.

The resulting evidence path is generated by those actions:

A_1, A_2, ... -> E_selected -> Y_conclusion.

This gives a stronger meaning to 窶徨esearch trajectory窶・without claiming access to hidden chain-of-thought.

Candidate Study C outcomes therefore include:
- first-action distribution;
- action-type frequencies;
- ordered evidence path;
- transition frequencies between action types;
- stopping-time distribution;
- propensity to inspect disconfirming or measurement-quality evidence.

A later model could estimate method-conditioned transition probabilities descriptively. A first-order Markov assumption must not be imposed unless tested.
